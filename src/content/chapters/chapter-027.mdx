<h1>Chapter 27</h1>

<p>I groped at my eye with one hand.</p>

<p>The heat beneath my fingers was searing.</p>

<p>I rubbed at it, but the dazzling gold reflected in the windowpane didn't fade.</p>

<p>What the hell is this?</p>

<p>I was frozen, panicking, when a sharp shout cut through.</p>

<p>"Trainee!"</p>

<p>The Chef Zombie was moving again.</p>

<p>Its leg crippled, the Chef Zombie crawled toward me across the floor, face-down.</p>

<p>'Crawled' wasn't quite the right word — its speed was so insane it was almost running.</p>

<p>I backed away, ejecting the magazine and clearing the chamber.</p>

<p>My hands trembled a little, but I didn't mess up.</p>

<p>The round from the chamber hit the steel floor with a hollow clang.</p>

<p>I shoved the Glock back into my sling bag and shouted.</p>

<p>"Please resume the repairs!"</p>

<p>I couldn't check on Mo Haein or Kwak Hanmuk — I had to keep my eyes on the Chef Zombie.</p>

<p>But I could tell the repairs had restarted.</p>

<p>The halted progress bar on the system window was climbing again.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Current Repair Progress: 69%</p></div>

<p>The Chef Zombie was noticeably slower than before, making it much easier to deal with.</p>

<p>But my own reaction speed had dropped too.</p>

<p>My mind was tangled up over the golden eyes, and the system window kept popping up, blocking my vision.</p>

<div class="systembox">
<p>
You feel a sense of alienation from yourself, but soon realize this is nothing.<br/>

Because you are ■■ ■.<br/>

This is merely a natural process of growth as a ■.
</p>
</div>

<div class="systembox">
<p>
However, ■■ ■ must still remain hidden.<br/>

To keep the secret, you believe that now is the most perfect — and most appropriate — time to register 'Mo Haein' and 'Kwak Hanmuk.'
</p>
</div>

<div class="systembox"><p>Would you like to register 'Mo Haein' and 'Kwak Hanmuk'?</p></div>

<p>I shut down every window except the repair progress.</p>

<p>First, I had to survive this.</p>

<p>Dead, it wouldn't matter what color my eyes flashed.</p>

<p>'Two jackstones left.'</p>

<p>I planned to save the last one.</p>

<p>I'd used the gun too — I couldn't afford any more situations that would drain the Chef Zombie's health.</p>

<p>But Phase 3 was about to start.</p>

<p>The moment I thought it, the progress bar climbed, as if it had been waiting.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Current Repair Progress: 70%</p></div>

<p>This was the trickiest stretch — from the moment the emergency generator repairs hit 70%.</p>

<p>The Chef Zombie, which had been crawling on the floor, suddenly began convulsing as it entered Phase 2.</p>

<p>"Grrrk... grrrk..."</p>

<p>Veins bulged across its body as it twisted in apparent agony.</p>

<p>The zombie's blue blood vessels swelled grotesquely, and it cracked its joints one by one — ttuk, ttuk, ttuk — as if testing a newborn body.</p>

<p>Then it slowly turned its head toward the emergency generator.</p>

<p>The generator, now 70% repaired, was roaring with noise.</p>

<p>"KIIIIEEEE!"</p>

<p>The Chef Zombie let out a high-pitched scream and crawled at full speed, its apron dragging across the floor.</p>

<p>Toward the emergency generator. Not me.</p>

<p>This was why Phase 2 was so difficult.</p>

<p>The Chef Zombie always attacks the emergency generator.</p>

<p>No matter how much aggro the player draws, it breaks free in seconds. Even using a jackstone only buys three seconds.</p>

<p>So in Phase 2, melee combat was the best — the only — strategy.</p>

<p>I quickly pulled the carabiner with the climbing rope from my sling bag and clipped it to a steel shelf.</p>

<p>Then I looped the rope around a thick pipe, crossing it, and finally tied it around my waist.</p>

<p>After checking the knot, I chased after the Chef Zombie.</p>

<p>And grabbed its leg.</p>

<p>The left leg — the one I'd shot in the knee, leaving it barely functional.</p>

<p>It was wearing rubber boots, so I almost just pulled the shoe off, but I quickly grabbed higher up and hugged the limb tight.</p>

<p>"KKEUGH! KKEEE!"</p>

<p>The Chef Zombie thrashed violently, but it didn't attack me.</p>

<p>In Phase 2, as long as the damage didn't exceed a certain threshold, the emergency generator remained the priority target.</p>

<p>Dragging me along, the Chef Zombie pushed forward with one leg and both arms.</p>

<p>I was yanked across the floor until the rope around my waist went taut.</p>

<p>"…!"</p>

<p>Pain crushed through my entire body, like I was being torn apart.</p>

<p>I gritted my teeth and planted my feet against a dented section of the steel floor.</p>

<p>A clammy sensation touched my skin.</p>

<p>The dark red fluid seeping from the Chef Zombie's leg was soaking into me.</p>

<p>Under normal circumstances, this strength would be beyond my ability to handle.</p>

<p>The rope was helping, barely, but I clearly couldn't hold out much longer.</p>

<p>'Just a little more…'</p>

<p>Sweat streaming down my face blurred my vision.</p>

<p>I blinked hard, barely clearing my sight, and finally saw the leading digit change.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Current Repair Progress: 90%</p></div>

<p>The moment repairs hit 90%, the Chef Zombie began convulsing.</p>

<p>Its whole body trembled, limbs twitching grotesquely.</p>

<p>A precursor to Phase 3.</p>

<p>In Phase 3, the Chef Zombie enters a berserk state.</p>

<p>Part of its finger bones grow into kitchen knife shapes. Speed and power both skyrocket. It unleashes a barrage of attacks across the entire generator room with its transformed body.</p>

<p>Its leg was injured, but judging by how fast it had been crawling earlier, the wound probably wouldn't reduce its berserk attack power much.</p>

<p>In other words, it would reach a level I couldn't handle.</p>

<p>This was happening because, originally, Kwak Hanmuk was supposed to fight the Chef Zombie.</p>

<p>But the situation had already spiraled, and I had to try something.</p>

<p>I pulled away from the Chef Zombie, tearing off my work gloves and tossing them aside.</p>

<p>Then I took out the fourth jackstone.</p>

<p>To attempt something I'd never been able to do in the game.</p>

<p>'Back then, it was physically impossible.'</p>

<p>But now — maybe it was possible.</p>

<p>I gripped the jackstone hard. And pried the plastic casing apart.</p>

<p>Click. The jackstone opened.</p>

<p>My nerves, wound tight, relaxed just a little.</p>

<p>In the original game, this item couldn't be disassembled.</p>

<p>When I checked the item, its description had changed too.</p>

<div class="systembox"><div class="systembox_wrap"><p>Broken Jackstone: PLEASE! Use this only for playing jackstones!</p></div></div>

<p>I poured the small metal filling fragments from inside the jackstone into my palm, then clenched them together with the plastic casing.</p>

<p>And I flung them in a scatter — away from the Chef Zombie, where its body transformation was about to begin.</p>

<p>The plastic casing and metal fragments scattered everywhere with faint sounds.</p>

<p>Compared to the diesel generator's roar, it was nothing more than the rustle of falling leaves.</p>

<p>But the Chef Zombie reacted instantly.</p>

<p>Its convulsing body froze. The physical transformation stopped.</p>

<p>The guttural cries that had been rattling from its throat vanished completely, leaving only the engine noise filling the generator room.</p>

<p>The Chef Zombie stood frozen, as if glitched — then slowly turned its head.</p>

<p>Its gaze landed on the scattered jackstone fragments.</p>

<p>"Ah... ah..."</p>

<p>A different sound escaped the Chef Zombie. Unlike anything before.</p>

<p>It shifted its gaze helplessly across the fragments scattered everywhere.</p>

<p>Its head jerked wildly — up, down, left, right.</p>

<p>Then, abruptly, it began to crawl.</p>

<p>Not toward the emergency generator. Not targeting me.</p>

<p>Just crawling everywhere to pick up the scattered jackstone fragments.</p>

<p>But its hands — already mid-transformation — were lumpy with protruding bone.</p>

<p>Designed solely for destruction, they couldn't pick up the tiny metal fillings.</p>

<p>At most, it managed to scoop up the plastic casing and shove it in its mouth.</p>

<p>While the Chef Zombie repeated futile motions trying to gather the fragments, the repair progress steadily climbed.</p>

<p>Finally, Kwak Hanmuk tightened the last screw.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Current Repair Progress: 100%</p></div>

<div class="systembox"><p>Emergency generator repairs complete!</p></div>

<p>A cheerful notification popped up along with the system window.</p>

<p>The moment I confirmed the completion window, Kwak Hanmuk grabbed the screwdriver in his hand and shot to his feet.</p>

<p>I blocked his path as he lunged toward the Chef Zombie.</p>

<p>No — tried to block.</p>

<p>"Captain Kwak — ugh!"</p>

<p>I'd forgotten about the rope around my waist.</p>

<p>I couldn't move forward and fell backward instead.</p>

<p>My tailbone throbbed from the hard landing, but I had no time to check and shouted.</p>

<p>"Don't kill it!"</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk, who had been about to stomp the writhing Chef Zombie's back and drive the screwdriver into its nape, looked at me.</p>

<p>His gaze was razor-sharp.</p>

<p>I froze for a moment, then slowly moved my lips.</p>

<p>"The jackstones you brought me — they belonged to the little one's father."</p>

<p>"So?"</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk asked, one foot still planted on the Chef Zombie's back.</p>

<p>"Is that a reason to let the boss zombie live?"</p>

<p>"We're going to develop a cure. It can be restored."</p>

<p>"Is it related to the True Ending?"</p>

<p>"…Yes."</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk's eyes were still fixed on me. I slowly added an explanation.</p>

<p>"The sub-quest you got at the elementary school — it connects to the True Ending."</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk grinned and tossed the screwdriver into the air.</p>

<p>It spun end over end before landing back in his grip.</p>

<p>"You should've said so from the start."</p>

<p>It's not actually related.</p>

<p>Whether the Chef Zombie dies here or not won't affect the development of the cure or the vaccine.</p>

<p>But I wanted to save it.</p>

<p>Because I'd played DeZonDeal countless times and never once succeeded at this.</p>

<div class="bigbox2_pixel"><p>◆ Sub-Quest: Finding the Little One's Father.</p></div>

<p>Accept the quest from the little one at the elementary school, and you receive a jackstone to use in the boss fight.</p>

<p>You're supposed to use the jackstone to defeat the boss — so the quest was doomed to fail.</p>

<p>I'd tried everything, wondering if there was a way to succeed — starting with not using the jackstone at all — but I failed every time.</p>

<p>All I ever got was the ending where the little one, hearing of their father's death, burst into tears and asked me to find him.</p>

<p>DeZonDeal might be a zombie apocalypse setting, but since I'd decided to make a cure this time, I wanted a more complete happy ending.</p>

<p>Of course, I can't save every zombie.</p>

<p>'But I can save one or two that I know.'</p>

<p>As long as it doesn't interfere with clearing the game.</p>

<p>And anyway — even if I don't kill it, as long as the generator repairs succeed, it counts as a victory.</p>

<p>I flicked my eyes toward the system window floating in the corner.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>You have won the battle against the Chef Zombie!</p></div>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk tossed the screwdriver and caught it again.</p>

<p>"Got a way to restrain this thing?"</p>

<p>"Yes. If you could just — keep it pinned a little longer, I'd appreciate it."</p>

<p>I quickly untied the climbing rope from my waist.</p>

<p>As I turned to unclip the carabiner from the steel shelf, Mo Haein blocked my path.</p>

<p>"Hold on."</p>

<p>She reached out toward me.</p>

<p>I stared at her approaching hand, tense.</p>

<p>Something I'd forgotten in the chaos with the Chef Zombie belatedly came back to me.</p>

<p>My eyes had flashed gold.</p>

<p>Mo Haein grabbed the zipper of the sling bag I wore across my chest and pulled it open.</p>

<p>She took out the pistol, pointed the muzzle at the floor, and pulled the trigger.</p>

<p>A click — the trigger locked back.</p>

<p>While she engaged the safety, I was still on edge.</p>

<p>But Mo Haein simply placed the pistol back in the sling bag.</p>

<p>'She's not arresting me this time…?'</p>

<p>After being arrested on charges of National Calamity before, I looked at her, slightly dazed.</p>

<p>Then, from the generator room speakers — a crackle of static.</p>

<p>Followed by a familiar voice.</p>
<br/>
<em><p>"U-ugh, R-Roasted Chestnut-nim…! A-are you alive…? Hic — if you're alive, could you please come quick…?"</p></em>
<br/>
<p>It was Yi Gaon's broadcast.</p>

<p>Before I could even be surprised that she'd turned on the broadcast, she dropped an even bigger bomb.</p>
<br/>
<em><p>"S-sob, hic — I think I killed someone…!"</p></em><br/>
