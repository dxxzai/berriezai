<h1>Chapter 112</h1>


<p>It was a title that slipped from someone’s lips at a moment I never expected.</p>

<p>Even though it was a name I had used for a long time, it felt incredibly unfamiliar.</p>

<p>I couldn't hide the wavering in my eyes.</p>

<p>Orca gently set me down on the ground.</p>

<p>He continued speaking, scrutinizing me with his pitch-black eyes.</p>

<p>“It was exactly like this. Your face, your actions…”</p>

<p>“…….”</p>

<p>“The dream was so vivid it felt like reality. I always felt a sense of loss whenever I woke up. Every night, I waited for Roasted Chestnut to appear in my dreams.”</p>

<p>“…….”</p>

<p>“The time spent with Roasted Chestnut was the only distraction in that wretched solitary cell. Haha, I used to look forward to bedtime so much.”</p>

<p>“…….”</p>

<p>“I desperately wished for it to be real, but I had given up, knowing it was merely a futile dream… yet here you are, appearing right before me!”</p>

<p>Orca burst into laughter once more.</p>

<p>“Perhaps all those dreams were actually premonitions.”</p>

<p>I had a suspicion.</p>

<p>It wasn't a premonition.</p>

<p>Orca had experienced the gameplay I had performed in the dream.</p>

<p>To be precise, it seemed he had experienced—through his dreams—the version of me that played the role of the medic in Cell-X.</p>

<p>It was a moment where reality and the game intersected.</p>

<p>I had experienced a moment like this before.</p>

<p>It was in &lt;Clean! Clean! Clean!&gt;</p>

<p>In Clean3, the butlers remembered every move I had made; the moment they saw me, they welcomed me by calling me 'Roasted Chestnut.'</p>

<p>However, the premise of Clean3 was that the game itself &quot;remembered the player's previous actions.&quot;</p>

<p>But Cell-X doesn't have a feature like that.</p>

<p>Why, then, did Orca dream about the gameplay we shared? I tried to think of what Clean3 and Cell-X might have in common, but nothing immediately came to mind.</p>

<p>I... looked at Orca.</p>

<p>I couldn't quite decide how to respond to the look of eager anticipation on his face.</p>

<p>It was because I didn't know how the fact that I 'knew' them would play out.</p>

<p>I had already been through this once before with Clean3.</p>

<p>The butlers there had acted on my behalf, driven by a deep fondness for me.</p>

<p>But their goodwill wasn't what I wanted.</p>

<p>I had nearly been infected and turned into a mansion butler myself, and even after barely regaining my senses, I was shipped off to CallOVo by them.</p>

<p>The result of that was Dominic, standing right beside me now.</p>

<p>There was no telling what might happen this time, either.</p>

<p>Of course, it was unlikely to be worse than the situation with Dominic...</p>

<p>Still, it was clearly dangerous. The conclusion was obvious without needing further thought.</p>

<p>'I shouldn't let them figure out that I know them.'</p>

<p>I calmly parted my lips.</p>

<p>I intended to say that I had no idea what Roasted Chestnut meant—that this was all completely new to me.</p>

<p>But just as I was about to speak, a tendril from Creature No. 1 slithered out and wrapped around my forearm.</p>

<p>I felt a gentle caress, as if it were trying to gauge my reaction.</p>

<p>The words died in my throat. My parted lips closed on their own.</p>

<p>Orca smiled at me as I stood there, rendered speechless.</p>

<p>&quot;We all dreamed of you, Roasted Chestnut. We waited for you to come to us.&quot;</p>

<p>Perhaps it was because I had already been through this with Clean3.</p>

<p>Now that reality and the game were merging, for some reason, I didn't feel much fear.</p>

<p>A flood of other emotions and thoughts kept rising up, completely burying any sense of fear. I had always been the only one who remembered.</p>

<p>I had been the only one reminiscing about and dwelling on the moments we shared.</p>

<p>But now, we shared the same memory.</p>

<p>Perhaps this time...</p>

<p>Just this once, maybe I could change the True Ending.</p>

<p>Meeting the affectionate gaze directed at me, I finally gave my answer.</p>

<p>“I had a dream, too.”</p>

<p>I might come to regret this answer.</p>

<p>But I felt I would regret it even more if I gave any other reply.</p>

<div class="bigbox2_pixel"><p>“We shall miss you, Roasted Chestnut. But I hope you do not miss us.”</p></div>

<div class="bigbox2_pixel"><p>“We are together, yet you are alone, aren't you? It seems so lonely... Please, think of us just a little. Ah, but you mustn't forget us entirely!”</p></div>

<p>“A dream featuring you, Orca, and the other creatures.”</p>

<p>I looked up at Orca with earnest eyes.</p>

<p>“That’s why I came here. I wanted to help. You might be suspicious, but could you trust me just this once? I...”</p>

<p>I bit my lip for a moment before continuing.</p>

<p>“I will give you your freedom.”</p>

<p>The smile that had been brightening Orca’s face vanished.</p>

<p>He could no longer smile.</p>

<p>“You... you are exactly like the one in my dream.”</p>

<p>He studied me with a cautious gaze.</p>

<p>“I had wanted to meet you so badly that I even wondered if this, too, was just a dream.”</p>

<p>How much of the journey had Orca shared with me in that dream?</p>

<p>Had he been there right up until the moment I reached the True Ending?</p>

<p>It didn't seem so.</p>

<p>If he had, he likely would have treated me differently than he was now.</p>

<p>Orca hesitated, then asked... “If you don’t mind, may I call you Roasted Chestnut?”</p>

<p>I nodded silently.</p>

<p>Orca extended his hand toward me.</p>

<p>As I clasped his outstretched hand, a smile finally returned to his face.</p>

<p>It seemed he had realized that this was reality, not a dream.</p>

<p>The warmth radiating from the hand of this creature—who possessed the nature of an orca—felt comforting.</p>

<p>Orca gripped my hand firmly and shook it.</p>

<p>“In any case, this is our first time meeting in reality, isn't it? I’m Orca. It’s a pleasure to meet you, Roasted Chestnut!”</p>

<p>“The pleasure is mine, Orca.”</p>

<p>Just as we were shaking hands...</p>

<p>BOOM!</p>

<p>A massive, thunderous roar erupted.</p>

<p>Startled by the vibration, Creature No. 1—forgetting its massive size—clung to me, trying to hide behind my back.</p>

<p>Orca, too, was visibly startled and turned his gaze toward the source of the sound.</p>

<p>A series of system windows popped up before my eyes.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Creature No. 3 has been subdued by a guard.</p></div>

<div class="systembox"><p>Creature No. 8 has been subdued by a guard.</p></div>

<div class="systembox"><p>Creature No. 10 has been...</p></div>

<p>The escaped creatures were being subdued by the guards.</p>

<p>I spoke quickly.</p>

<p>“The others have been captured.”</p>

<p>“How did you know that?”</p>

<p>“I’ll... explain later. Orca, I have a plan.”</p>

<p>I hurriedly outlined my plan to him.</p>

<p>He seemed taken aback by the rapid-fire explanation, but true to the sharp intellect of an orca, he grasped it immediately.</p>

<p>“I’m counting on you. I’m going to check on the others who haven’t been subdued yet.” </p>
<p>“Understood. Don’t worry. But… will Roasted Chestnut be alright?”</p>

<p>Orca asked with a look of concern.</p>

<p>In truth, it didn’t seem like it would go well. But for now, there was no other option.</p>

<p>I quickly soothed Creature No. 1—which was clinging to my back and refusing to let go—before speaking.</p>

<p>“I’m fine. I’ll be going now.”</p>

<p>I sprinted straight toward the exercise yard and hurriedly sent a message to Je-Hyun-oh.</p>

<p>I checked the sky, but Je-Hyun-oh—who had been flying around just moments ago—was nowhere to be seen.</p>

<p>‘Where did he go?’</p>

<p>There was no way he could have been captured, yet I couldn't fathom why he had vanished.</p>

<p>Just then, a system window popped up again.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Creature No. 11 has been subdued by the prison guards.</p></div>

<p>I picked up my pace.</p>

<p>I headed toward the place where Creature No. 12—who hadn't been caught yet—might have fled.</p>

<p>I stopped in my tracks while rushing toward the coastal cliffs.</p>

<p>A creature with the lower body of a spider and the upper body of a woman was being hauled up the cliff, wrapped in countless chains.</p>

<p>No. 12 had already been discovered.</p>

<p>‘How did they find her?’</p>

<p>I couldn't believe the guards had pinpointed No. 12's location so quickly.</p>

<p>No, it wasn't just No. 12.</p>

<p>The other creatures were also being subdued, as if their escape routes had all been predicted.</p>

<p>Against the backdrop of her struggling form, the system window displayed a cruel notification.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Creature No. 12 has been subdued by the prison guards.</p></div>
<div class="systembox">
<p>
You have failed the quest: ‘Find the Escaped Creatures.’
</p>
</div>

<p>Upon seeing the quest failure notification, Dominic, who had been quietly watching, spoke up in surprise.</p> <p>“Roasted Chestnut. I see a notification that you’ve failed. Does this mean you’ll be tormented by the guards now?”</p>

<p>But I couldn't even bring myself to properly look at the words announcing the quest failure.</p>

<p>No. 12 was thrashing in agony, likely due to the pain from the chains.</p>

<p>One of the guards was sent flying by a spider-like leg that flailed wildly during the struggle.</p>

<p>Another guard immediately drew his weapon and cocked it.</p>

<p>It was a gun designed to kill creatures.</p>

<p>Frozen in place, I hurriedly dashed forward.</p>

<p>“Wait!”</p>

<p>Only when I reached the edge of the cliff did I realize the man holding the gun was the Chief of Security.</p>

<p>Panting heavily, I stepped in front of Subject 12.</p>

<p>The lips beneath his gray metal mask parted slowly.</p>

<p>“Little Lamb.”</p>

<p>He spoke to me in a chilling voice.</p>

<p>“It seems you failed to become a guard.”</p>

<p>The Chief of Security realized I hadn't become his colleague the moment he saw me.</p>

<p>I was wearing the guard’s uniform, but I wasn't wearing the metal mask.</p>

<p>My appearance made it obvious that I hadn't completed the rookie guard training course.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Suspicion +10</p></div>

<p>“How did you escape the training grounds?”</p>

<p>The Chief of Security asked the question as the suspicion meter rose.</p>

<p>I was gasping for air, having run non-stop to get here.</p>

<p>I steadied my breathing as best I could before speaking.</p>

<p>“Chief of Security. Please, do not execute the creatures.”</p>

<p>“You didn't answer my question, Little Lamb.”</p>

<p>“At the training grounds…”</p>

<p>Just as I was about to offer an excuse...</p>

<p>Static—a voice crackled over the radio.</p>

<p>—Did you find the medic? </p>
<p>The poor audio quality of the walkie-talkie made it impossible to identify the speaker, but the fact that they were speaking down to the Chief of Security was startling.</p>

<p>Given the Chief of Security’s position within the prison, there was no one who would speak to him in such a condescending manner.</p>

<p>Except for one person.</p>

<p>The Warden.</p>

<p>While playing Cell-X, one rarely encounters the Warden.</p>

<p>Since the Chief of Security held the actual power in the prison, interactions with him were far more frequent.</p>

<p>“I’ve found the Medic. I’m bringing him in right now.”</p>

<p>While I stood there blinking in bewilderment, the Chief of Security gestured to the guards.</p>

<p>Two guards grabbed me by the arms, and I was promptly dragged off to the Warden’s office.</p>

<p>‘Well, at least I stopped the execution.’</p>

<p>With the Warden summoning me, the plan to kill Creature No. 12 had been put on hold.</p>


<p>‘But how did the guards pinpoint the creature’s location?’</p>

<p>Even I, having played the game, didn’t know the creature’s exact location when the prison break event triggered.</p>

<p>The standard strategy was to guess which of the possible locations it might be in and check them one by one, or to get help from another creature.</p>

<p>Yet now, it felt as though the guards knew every single place the creature could possibly go.</p>

<p>I wondered if it was some kind of anomaly, but it still didn’t make sense.</p>

<p>‘It’s also strange that the warden is looking for the Medic.’</p>

<p>With so many errors occurring, I couldn't even figure out where the problem lay.</p>

<p>I racked my brain as the guards dragged me along.</p>

<p>The door to the warden’s office was made of a material that looked noticeably high-quality compared to the rest of the prison.</p>

<p>After a brief knock, the Chief of Security spoke in a respectful tone.</p>

<p>“We’re coming in.”</p>

<p>Then, he opened the door.</p>

<p>The warden’s office was furnished with high-end pieces that matched the quality of the door.</p>

<p>Beyond a spacious desk, I saw a man sitting with his back to us.</p>

<p>He wore a uniform that differed slightly from those of the regular guards.</p>

<p>A cape draped over one shoulder of his uniform was a distinctive feature.</p>

<p>The Chief of Security saluted and spoke.</p>

<p>“I’ve brought the Medic you asked for.”</p>

<p>The chair slowly swiveled around.</p>

<p>When I came face-to-face with the warden, my jaw dropped.</p>

<p>He… wasn’t wearing a metal mask.</p>

<p>What covered the warden’s face was a white mask of cognitive suppression.</p>
