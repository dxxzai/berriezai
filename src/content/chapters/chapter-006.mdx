<h1>Chapter 6</h1>

<p>Before I could answer, she spat out coldly.</p><p></p>

<p>"Nut Candy."</p>

<p>An interrogation that said: don't even think about weaseling your way out. Her words brought a scene flashing through my mind.</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">"Well, when I picked up this candy, it said it could repair toys."</span></p>
<br/>
<p>My explanation when I'd been talking about collecting the Sad Smiley pieces.</p>

<p>Non-Adaptees can't see the Nut Candy's item description — the System window.</p>

<p>Simple logic, but to me, the existence of the System window was so obvious I'd slipped up.</p>

<p>Mo Haein had been suspicious ever since then. Seeing me alive and well after being left alone must have confirmed it.</p>

<p>I'll have to be more careful from now on.</p>

<p>Not that it mattered. I'd already decided to stop pretending to be a civilian.</p>

<p>Playing dumb wouldn't work — not with Park Seonggyeon in the picture. The true ending would be impossible.</p>

<p>That was why I'd dealt with Princess Tarantella in the first place.</p>

<p>I gave a deliberately vague answer.</p>

<p>"It seems like I can see it."</p>

<p>"Seems like?"</p>

<p>"This is my first time experiencing something like this. A rectangular message pops up — is that what you call a System window?"</p>

<p>"Yeah."</p>

<p>Mo Haein still hadn't lowered her spear. She asked,</p>

<p>"How'd you deal with the spider?"</p>

<p>"Just dodged it."</p>

<p>The tip of her spear jabbed at the underside of my chin. I quickly added,</p>

<p>"I could see its pattern. I've got pretty good eyesight."</p>

<p>Mo Haein let out a disbelieving laugh.</p>

<p>"You're telling me you beat the spider with nothing but dynamic vision and reaction speed? No items?"</p>

<p>"I figured it was like a game, so I played it like one. I like games."</p>

<p>My eyes naturally slant upward. If I keep my face blank, people tend to misunderstand me.</p>

<p>I tried my best to make my eyes look gentle, even pulling the corners of my mouth up slightly.</p>

<p>"Besides, you said we don't actually die, right?"</p>

<p>"Kid's got no sense of fear."</p>

<p>Mo Haein muttered something that might have been an insult or not, then finally lowered her spear.</p>

<p>I rubbed my jaw where she'd jabbed me. Didn't seem to be bleeding.</p>

<p>Mo Haein stared at me silently.</p>

<p>"An Adaptee entering a Trial for the first time, pulling off a play like this..."</p>

<p>She trailed off, mouth snapping shut.</p>

<p>"Let's talk outside first. What's your name?"</p>

<p>"Han Goyo."</p>

<p>"Call me Captain."</p>

<p>"Yes."</p>

<p>And then, in the next moment, I found myself calling out to her.</p>

<p>"...Captain?"</p>

<p>Click.</p>

<p>Hinge handcuffs snapped around both my wrists.</p>

<p>Adaptee Restraint Cuffs — a special-issue item from the Trial Response Agency, only given to officers ranked Captain or higher.</p>

<p>Completely unable to move my hands, I stared at Mo Haein in shock.</p>

<p>She ran her hand along the Black Moon Blade and warned,</p>

<p>"Behave yourself."</p>

<p>"..."</p>

<p>I didn't ask or argue. I just shut my mouth.</p>

<p>Looked like winning Mo Haein's trust was a bust.</p>

<p>With me cuffed and walking ahead of her, Mo Haein didn't head for the next stage. She turned her steps back.</p>

<p>"We're going to find Park Seonggyeon."</p>

<p>Mo Haein opened a sealed door in the corridor between the Warehouse and the Laboratory. A small space with an elevator appeared.</p>

<p>Unlike the outside — decorated in bright colors like a children's paradise — this elevator was made of stark, colorless steel.</p>
<br/>
<div class="fieldset"><p>▼</p></div>
<br/>
<p>The elevator only had a down button. I pressed it, and the doors opened immediately.</p>

<p>Bright red lettering, scrawled large across the wall, caught my eye first.</p>

<p style="color:red; text-align: center; font-size: 27px;">CHILDREN NOT ALLOWED!</p>

<p>Inside, the floor buttons were just as sparse as the outside. To be precise, in a space meant for about fifty buttons, there was only one — oversized.</p>

<p>Inside a long rectangle, a dense string of numbers was crammed together.</p>

<p></p><div class="fieldset">B9999999999999999....</div><p></p>

<p>I tried counting the nines, then gave up. Mo Haein spread her fingers wide and pressed the giant button.</p>

<p>A crackling announcement came through.</p>
<br/>
<em><p>"G-g-going d-d-down. Ch-children, children, d-down. Down. Down."</p></em>
<br/>
<p>The doors closed, and the elevator began to move.</p>

<p>Inside the descending car, she hooked her spear over her arm and crossed them.</p>

<p>"You."</p>

<p>Suspicious criminal candidate or not, she was still talking to me. I answered cheerfully.</p>

<p>"Yes."</p>

<p>"What do you think happens if you die here?"</p>

<p>When you exhaust all your given lives and game over — or when you equip Cherry Pickers on both arms and both legs.</p>

<p>The Player becomes an employee of the Factory.</p>

<p>"You become a worker in the toy factory, unable to leave forever."</p>

<p>Mo Haein was telling me something I already knew.</p>

<p>But what she said next was something I didn't.</p>

<p>"The place we're going now — there are people trapped in the Trial there. In here."</p>

<p>I'd already seen what was on the underground floor in the game.</p>

<p>Hundreds of employees in the same work uniforms as the Player, working hard to make toys.</p>

<p>I'd checked every corner, but there were no events, so I'd figured it was a hidden space the creator added as a light joke — an easter egg.</p>

<p>But all of them were people trapped in the Trial?</p>

<p>The memory surfaced: tiny dot-graphic employees repeating the same motions, making toys. I'd thought it was cute.</p>

<p>They never tired. Never got sick. Never felt pain.</p>

<p>In the gloomy, dark underground factory — so different from the surface — they just made toys with bright, smiling faces, perfectly matching Happy Smile Factory.</p>

<p>I slowly swallowed and opened my mouth.</p>

<p>"Is Lieutenant Park Seonggyeon there?"</p>

<p>"Lieutenant my ass. I'll have that bastard stripped of his uniform."</p>

<p>Mo Haein leaned her face against the Black Moon Blade. Her cheek pressed into it, puffing out slightly. For a moment I forgot the situation and just found it fascinating.</p>

<p>They said the Black Moon Blade wouldn't cut its owner — and sure enough, even with her face pressed against that sharp blade, her cheek was perfectly fine, not a single scratch.</p>

<p>She lowered her eyes and moved her lips.</p>

<p>"From the start, he attacks an NPC, goes hostile with all the toys, stages a mutiny against his superior — and doesn't even have the ability to clear the stage on his own."</p>

<p>So Park Seonggyeon had attacked Smiley.</p>

<p>And Mo Haein had been trying to protect him, which cost her hearts.</p>

<p>I finally understood what that noise was, back when I first woke up in the Factory.</p>

<p>Mo Haein — revived after her first death — had been dealing with the Smileys that had already turned hostile.</p>

<p>That was why a veteran like her ended up on the hard collapse-ending route.</p>

<p>"This underground area — it's the only place Park Seonggyeon can hide safely without getting attacked by the toys until I clear the stage."</p>

<p>I looked at her name tag. The smiley emoticon on it was crying now.</p>

<p>Mo Haein was on the verge of game over.</p>

<p>"Isn't that dangerous?"</p>

<p>"Of course it is. But I can't leave a loose end at my back until I clear it, can I?"</p>

<p>Fair point. Park Seonggyeon might launch a surprise attack.</p>

<p>Park Seonggyeon had 2 hearts left.</p>

<p>Sure, if he equipped Cherry Pickers on all four limbs, he'd game over instantly — but he wasn't crazy enough to do that.</p>

<p>With three Cherry Pickers equipped, Park Seonggyeon had the physical advantage in the current situation.</p>

<p>Not that I didn't have my own contingency.</p>

<p>The Bolt Candy — I'd bought time with buggy play to get it.</p>

<p>If things went south, I planned to hand the Bolt Candy to Mo Haein.</p>

<p>Explaining how I'd obtained it would be tricky, so dealing with Park Seonggyeon without it would be ideal, but still.</p>

<p>Thump.</p>

<p>The elevator reached its destination, stopping with a short jolt.</p>
<br/>
<em><p>"■■ floor. Ch-children, children, children, please do not get off."</p></em>
<br/>
<p>The doors opened.</p>

<p>But what greeted me wasn't what I'd expected.</p>

<p>A bright yellow face filled my vision.</p>

<p>Smiley...</p>

<p>A Smiley so enormous it filled the elevator doorway with its face, waiting for me.</p>

<p>Its plastic eyes — smooth, pitch-black, no pupils — reflected Mo Haein and me.</p>

<p>Both of us frozen, eyes wide.</p>

<p>We couldn't even breathe, let alone scream.</p>

<p>Like one wrong breath would get us crushed on the spot.</p>

<p>In HapFactory, Smiley's giant form only appeared in the final boss fight.</p>

<p>The giant Smiley had difficult attack patterns befitting a final boss — large-scale area attacks using its massive body and various toys.</p>

<p>You were supposed to face it with full preparation.</p>

<p>Why was it here?</p>

<p>This was a space where toys weren't supposed to appear.</p>

<p>Then again, who said they couldn't?</p>

<p>The game was already diverging from what I knew. This was entirely possible.</p>

<p>What I needed to think about wasn't the cause.</p>

<p>It was how to deal with the giant Smiley right in front of me.</p>

<p>"..."</p>

<p>Mo Haein lifted her foot very slowly.</p>

<p>And stepped back.</p>

<p>A cautious step.</p>

<p>I could see the blue veins bulging on the back of her hand as she gripped the Black Moon Blade.</p>

<p>Luckily, even as Mo Haein took a step back, Smiley didn't move.</p>

<p>She silently signaled me with her eyes.</p>

<p>I started backing away, just as slowly as Mo Haein.</p>

<p>I tried to.</p>

<p>Shit.</p>

<p>Krrrrrk. The elevator doors, forced open, let out an unsettling sound.</p>

<p>Smiley had pushed its face inside.</p>

<p>Now it was clear. The one Smiley was interested in was me.</p>

<p>Cold sweat trickled down Mo Haein's temple.</p>

<p>HapFactory's final boss was no joke, even for Mo Haein.</p>

<p>If she were alone, she might have been able to attack the giant Smiley with the Black Moon Blade and win.</p>

<p>But with a burden to protect? Impossible.</p>

<p>The cramped elevator — too narrow to swing a long spear like the Black Moon Blade freely — didn't help either.</p>

<p>At this rate, Mo Haein might game over.</p>

<p>She didn't know me, but I knew her.</p>

<p>I didn't want to see someone I'd grown fond of through Archive's games end up in an ending where they became a toy factory employee.</p>

<p>I decided to take a gamble.</p>

<p>I moved my foot forward — the one I'd been pulling back.</p>

<p>As I took a step forward, Mo Haein's eyes went wide.</p>

<p>She looked like she wanted to yell at me, but afraid of provoking Smiley, she could only move her lips.</p>

<p>I took another step forward.</p>

<p>And another.</p>

<p>Smiley pulled its face back.</p>

<p>The elevator doors — which I'd thought were broken — rattled and tried to close again.</p>

<p>CRUNCH. Smiley ripped off the elevator door panel with its claw hand.</p>

<p>Thanks to that, I could step out easily.</p>

<p>Though it would've been better if it had just let the doors close...</p>

<p>Smiley backed away slowly, matching my pace.</p>

<p>As I walked, I pulled the Bolt Candy from my inventory and held it in my hand.</p>

<p>I let it drop quietly, then stepped completely out of the elevator.</p>

<p>The underground floor — hidden behind Smiley — came into view.</p>

<p>A space so wide and tall it was hard to grasp the scale at a glance.</p>

<p>The deeper parts were hidden in darkness, but I was sure they stretched far.</p>

<p>Dozens of massive machines sat there, eerily silent.</p>

<p>All of them seemed powered off, completely still.</p>

<p>And hundreds of employees.</p>

<p>In colorful work uniforms, they stood at attention with wide smiles — their mouths stretched to the limit.</p>

<p>Despite so many people present, not a single breath could be heard.</p>

<p>Probably because they weren't 'people' anymore.</p>

<p>The giant Smiley, with the employees lined up behind it, looked down at me.</p>

<p>...Kinda scary, I'll admit.</p>

<p>A toy about fifteen meters tall was quite imposing.</p>

<p>Still, I was okay for now.</p>

<p>Judging by how HapFactory's toys had treated me so far — with goodwill — Smiley probably wouldn't attack first either.</p>

<p>Smiley's claw hand reached toward me. I didn't feel any hostile intent, so I waited.</p>

<p>The claw grabbed me like a claw machine grabbing a prize.</p>

<p>I was lifted up like a prize doll.</p>

<p>Smiley brought me to its face. A mechanical voice came through.</p>
<br/>
<em><p>"Smiley-welcomes-you-only-you-in-Happy-Smile-Factory-we-together-happy-happy-."</p></em>
<br/>
<p>Behind the mechanical voice repeating "happy happy," I could hear children's laughter and cheers.</p>

<p>An eerie chill ran down my spine.</p>

<p>The moment Smiley put even a little pressure in its hand, I'd break apart into blocks.</p>

<p>But strangely, I wasn't that afraid.</p>

<p>Maybe because Smiley — like Mo Haein — was also an NPC I liked.</p>

<p>Even at this point, I still wanted to end it with the true ending.</p>

<p>It might be impossible. But I wanted to try until it worked.</p>

<p>"Smiley."</p>

<p>I smiled at Smiley.</p>

<p>"Want me to kill you?"</p>

<p>The absolute condition for the true ending — Smiley's death.</p>

<p>I'd spoken the wish HapFactory's final boss desperately wanted. Now I waited for its answer.</p>
