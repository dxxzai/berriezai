<h1>Chapter 14</h1>

<p>I stared at him, eyes wide. Kwak Hanmuk frowned.</p>

<p>"Captain Mo looks like that, but she's a real hard-ass. Unless she's infected, there's no reason she wouldn't have made it to the Green Zone."</p>

<p>His words clicked immediately.</p>

<p>Mo Haein was the type who valued rules, and TRA's top priority recommendation for Trial entry was regrouping with your team.</p>

<p>So she would have made joining up with us her primary goal.</p>

<p>Even if she ran into civilians caught up in the Trial, odds were she wouldn't have acted alone.</p>

<p>Barring truly unavoidable circumstances.</p>

<p>When I tried to imagine a reason critical enough to keep Mo Haein from entering the Green Zone, zombie infection came to mind right away.</p>

<p>But a TRA team leader wasn't a position you got handed for free.</p>

<p>She could escape even a horde of Dead Zombies.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk seemed lost in the same question, deep in thought.</p>

<p>I asked carefully.</p>

<p>"What if she ran into Lieutenant Colonel Je Hyun-oh?"</p>

<p>Maybe a clash with Je Hyun-oh — who was aggressive toward Adaptees — had occurred.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk groaned at my suggestion.</p>

<p>"Well... it's possible, I guess. But probably not? The lieutenant colonel kind of cuts us some slack."</p>

<p>"What do you mean by 'cuts us some slack'?"</p>

<p>"He doesn't attack us."</p>

<p>"...Ah."</p>

<p>True enough — even in the game, he hadn't attacked TRA personnel.</p>

<p>Either way, if she'd actually been bitten by a zombie, things were complicated.</p>

<p>In DeZonDeal, getting bitten by a zombie turns you into a Dead Zombie. The principle was immediate termination, given how dangerous the infected were.</p>

<p>A True Ending would fix it, but Je Hyun-oh is a variable...</p>

<p>I didn't know what he'd done here, so I couldn't be absolutely sure about the True Ending.</p>

<p>Just as I was sorting through the tangled thoughts, an angry voice rang out.</p>

<p>"Are you not interested in talking?!"</p>

<p>I'd completely forgotten about the people I'd parked in front of me. I belatedly turned my gaze to the middle-aged man.</p>

<p>The Green Zone housed about thirty people, including non-combatants like the elderly and children.</p>

<p>But everyone was too busy clinging to their own lives to risk stepping outside the Green Zone.</p>

<p>With food hard to come by, and me showing up with a backpack full of supplies, they were practically foaming at the mouth.</p>

<p>I pulled out the salmon mayo triangle kimbap — the one whose description made me most uneasy — and tossed it over the iron gate.</p>

<p>Just one triangle kimbap and they swarmed like locusts.</p>

<p>The wrapper burst, rice and seaweed completely separated, but that wasn't their concern.</p>

<p>Everyone was scrambling to get even a single grain of rice into their mouths.</p>

<p>"It's real food."</p>

<p>Even the middle-aged man, who'd been maintaining some dignity, couldn't hide his excitement as he chewed with rice grains stuck to his lips.</p>

<p>If Kwak Hanmuk and I pulled out a hot dog here, they'd probably faint on the spot.</p>

<p>I tapped my backpack, meeting their glittering eyes.</p>

<p>"Let me inside for a bit, and I'll tell you where to find supplies. Way more than this."</p>

<p>They exchanged glances for a long moment, then finally opened the iron gate.</p>

<p>But the peaceful development didn't last. The middle-aged man — apparently the leader of this Green Zone — cleared his throat repeatedly.</p>

<p>"Ahem. We'll let you into our safe zone too."</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk let out a hollow laugh at the condescending attitude, just as the leader said,</p>

<p>"But first, share that backpack."</p>

<p>The gun was still aimed at us.</p>

<p>They figured we'd managed to get food but no firearms, and they were pushing their luck.</p>

<p>I'd expected this, so I looked at Kwak Hanmuk.</p>

<p>He remembered the favor I'd asked before entering the Green Zone, and he executed it flawlessly.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk shoved his hand into his baseball jacket pocket and spat out a curse.</p>

<p>"Ah, shit."</p>

<p>"What did you just say?"</p>

<p>"Oh, sorry. Your hearing's not what it used to be."</p>

<p>He reached the leader in two strides and bent forward.</p>

<p>Tall and broad-shouldered, Kwak Hanmuk had an imposing build.</p>

<p>The leader, intimidated, hunched without realizing it.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk looked down at him and repeated himself.</p>

<p>"I said shit."</p>

<p>"Wha— what the hell, you insolent bastard—!"</p>

<p>The leader's face flushed red as he roared.</p>

<p>In that instant.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk pressed a pistol to his head. The leader froze solid at the feel of the muzzle pushing firmly against his skull.</p>

<p>I spoke in a calm voice.</p>

<p>"We're already carrying several firearms. I don't want to waste time."</p>

<p>Ziiip.</p>

<p>I opened the duffel bag, spreading its mouth wide.</p>

<p>After letting them get a good look at the packed contents — pistols, submachine guns, ammo, knives, axes — I asked,</p>

<p>"Still want to keep going?"</p>

<p>"I... I suppose I won't... I think I got a little carried away..."</p>

<p>The leader stammered out a vague apology.</p>

<p>Since I had no intention of taking over the Green Zone, I accepted the apology there.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk slipped back to my side and took the duffel bag.</p>

<p>He'd been busy dealing with zombies on the way here, so I'd carried the luggage alone. Now he took even the backpack from me and whined.</p>

<p>"To think Goyo hyung-nim had to carry all the luggage..."</p>

<p>Hearing a man seven years older than me keep calling me hyung-nim made me deeply uncomfortable.</p>

<p>I told him to just call me Goyo, then stated the purpose of our Green Zone entry.</p>

<p>"There are items I need to pick up here."</p>

<p>We were planning to set up the Special Mission Team's temporary base in the Yellow Zone.</p>

<p>The goal was to minimize travel time to the lab by establishing a base as close to the Dead Zone as possible.</p>

<p>To do that, I needed to sweep up the items obtainable from the Green Zone first.</p>

<p>"Let's split up and farm."</p>

<p>We also had to go find Mo Haein, so we needed to move faster than planned.</p>

<p>I told Kwak Hanmuk the items he needed and their locations.</p>

<p>As he listened to the item list, one eyebrow shot up at a particular item.</p>

<p>"That one's the most important."</p>

<p>Honestly, even I could see how weird it sounded without context.</p>

<p>But it was genuinely crucial for the True Ending.</p>

<p>"I'm counting on you."</p>

<p>"Alright, if hyung-nim says so, I'll do it."</p>

<p>"...Just call me Goyo."</p>

<p>"If Goyo says so, I'll do it."</p>

<p>"Right..."</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk was to check the elementary school — the survivor shelter — while I checked the broadcasting station within the Green Zone.</p>

<p>The station wasn't far from the school, so I could handle it alone. The Green Zone was safe, anyway.</p>

<p>I sent Kwak Hanmuk — backpack and duffel bag in tow — into the elementary school and headed for the broadcasting station.</p>

<p>The city wasn't large, so the station building was modest.</p>

<p>If not for the radio tower rising high above the building, I wouldn't have recognized it as a broadcasting station.</p>

<p>The eerie atmosphere of an abandoned building mixed with the darkness of night.</p>

<p>I quickened my pace and stepped inside.</p>

<p>Ding. A system window appeared.</p>

<div class="systembox"><div class="systembox_wrap_j"><p>◆Main Quest...</p></div></div>

<p>...It tried to appear, then stopped.</p>

<p>What the hell was this?</p>

<p>I waited for the system window for a while.</p>

<p>The square flickered, half-emerging and faltering, as if locked in an invisible struggle.</p>

<p>Then suddenly — thwump — it burst open.</p>

<div class="systembox">
<div class="systembox_wrap_j">
<p>
★Event Quest: Exciting Broadcasting Station Exploration <br/>

You have visited the broadcasting station!<br/>

The broadcasting station is ■■ ■■■'s minion! A tool of mass communication!<br/>

In the flood of infodemics, are you truly a ■■ with media literacy?<br/>

■■ ■■■ will keep watching you!
</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>...The tone was different somehow?</p>

<p>The content was strange too.</p>

<p>So what's the actual quest?</p>

<p>Maybe it was just exploring the station, but even that seemed weird.</p>

<p>Because this quest didn't exist in DeZonDeal.</p>

<p>I usually liked discovering new parts that weren't in the game, but this one felt off.</p>

<p>For now, I decided the system window had glitched out and ignored it.</p>

<p>I didn't have time to dwell on that. I needed to grab the items and find Mo Haein.</p>

<p>The broadcasting station was no exception to the zombie outbreak — the inside was a wreck.</p>

<p>I crossed the silent lobby and entered the hallway. No moonlight reached inside; the building was pitch black.</p>

<p>The station's interior was complicated, but I'd been in and out of this place so many times in the game that navigation wasn't hard.</p>

<p>Relying on a single flashlight beam, I fumbled my way toward the emergency stairs.</p>

<p>Just before turning the corner in the hallway.</p>

<p>"Grrraaaah!"</p>

<p>A zombie with tattered flesh lunged out.</p>

<p>I wasn't the type to get startled by jump scares. I calmly swung the baseball bat in my hand.</p>

<p>THWACK!</p>

<p>The zombie, struck by the bat, went flying.</p>

<p>Since it was a Green Zone zombie, even at night, one was manageable.</p>

<p>I left the twitching thing behind and found the emergency stairs.</p>

<p>"...Huh."</p>

<p>But something was off around the emergency exit door.</p>

<p>A row of green emergency exit signs — little running pictogram men — lined the hallway. All pointing in the same direction.</p>

<p>Except the direction was strange.</p>

<p>They weren't pointing toward the emergency stairs. They were pointing the opposite way.</p>

<p>It was an unsettling sight, but I had no choice. I walked against the flow of green pictogram men.</p>

<p>As I entered the stairwell, the first thing that caught my eye was the unusual floor indicator.</p>

<div class="fieldset"><p>⇧2☺⇩B1</p></div>

<p>A smiley face marked the current floor — the first floor. My destination, the live studio, was on the sixth floor.</p>

<p>I climbed the stairs briskly and checked the sign again on the sixth floor.</p>

<div class="fieldset"><p>⇧99☹⇩5</p></div>

<p>The face had been smiling the whole way up, but on the sixth floor, it was frowning. And the next floor was marked as 99.</p>

<p>I studied the sign carefully.</p>

<p>This wasn't in the game either.</p>

<p>In the game, it was just a normal emergency exit. No backward-pointing exit signs or weird smiley indicators.</p>

<p>Which made sense.</p>

<p>It's a zombie game.</p>

<p>This kind of thing didn't fit a zombie apocalypse setting. It wasn't something you'd see in &lt;Dead Zone Delivery&gt;.</p>

<p>&lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt;, maybe...</p>

<p>I gripped my baseball bat and cracked the emergency door open.</p>

<p>Flashlight beam stretched long into the hallway beyond. Nothing unusual.</p>

<p>I cautiously entered the sixth floor.</p>

<p>Among the three studios on this floor, I found the news studio.</p>

<p>The door was wide open, and the interior was a mess. Signs of a hasty escape.</p>

<p>Dried bloodstains on news scripts scattered everywhere.</p>

<p>I stepped over a fallen broadcast camera and headed toward the monitoring area.</p>

<p>I needed to find a secure USB among the cluster of monitors there.</p>

<p>Just as I was about to shine my flashlight — the room suddenly lit up, and an anchor's voice rang out.</p>
<br/>
<p><em>"A virus of unknown cause is occurring simultaneously nationwide."</em></p>

<p><em>"The Disease Control Agency reports that infected individuals show high fever, extreme aggression, and temporary cognitive decline."</em></p>
<br/>
<p>The monitors had turned on by themselves, playing news footage.</p><p></p>

<p>I wasn't surprised. I watched the screen.</p>

<p>In the game, the monitors turned on the same way, delivering DeZonDeal's backstory.</p>
<br/>
<p>
<em>"The government will announce specific countermeasures during an emergency briefing at 6 PM this evening."</em>
</p>

<p>
<em>"Citizens, please do not be misled by unverified information. Confirm accurate details through official announcements."</em>
</p>
<br/>
<p>A faint tremor lurked beneath the calm voice. Fear. Dread.</p>

<p>The screen changed. The anchor's premonition had been correct.</p>
<br/>
<em><p>"Citizens. We are relaying the government's emergency disaster order."</p></em>
<br/>
<p>Now the anchor's voice was nearly a scream.</p>
<br/>
<em><p>"The unidentified infection crisis currently spreading nationwide has exceeded controllable limits. Effective 6 PM today, the government issues an immediate nationwide evacuation order — no, please, help me, AAAARGH!"</p></em>
<br/>
<p>The news ended with the male and female anchors being torn apart by zombies side by side.</p>

<p>Leaving their screams behind, I found the USB in a drawer.</p>

<p>I pocketed it and was about to leave the studio when everything turned red.</p>

<p>I looked at the monitors. They were all displaying the same screen.</p>

<p>A blood-red breaking news screen.</p>
<div class="R_news_wrapper">
<p>Breaking News!</p>
</div>
<p>In that moment, overwhelmed by the red, the screen changed.</p>

<p>The new image that appeared was...</p>

<p>Me.</p>

<p>My face, slightly pale, looking up at the monitor, was reflected on screen.</p>

<p>Was it Samra?</p>

<p>But Samra couldn't enter the Trial, and likewise couldn't exert influence here.</p>

<p>I tensed at the incomprehensible situation.</p>

<p>KABOOM!</p>

<p>A deafening explosion rocked the building.</p>

<p>I staggered, barely catching myself before falling.</p>

<p>Ceiling and floor — both were covered in cracks.</p>

<p>The monitors that had been showing that strange footage were all shattered, their panels bleeding rainbow colors.</p>

<p>The impact came from above.</p>

<p>After a moment's hesitation, I left the broken monitors and ran for the emergency stairs.</p>

<p>The building could collapse any second.</p>

<p>But instead of running down, I went up. I took the stairs two or three at a time, climbing to the roof.</p>

<p>I'd wondered if something strange would happen since it said 99 floors, but thankfully I reached the rooftop safely.</p>
<div class="fieldset"><p>☹☹☹☹☹</p></div>
<p>The floor markings were a bit weird, though.</p>

<p>I threw open the roof door.</p>

<p>...Crazy.</p>

<p>The scene beyond the open door made me curse under my breath.</p>

<p>The radio tower, once soaring high above the roof, was folded in half.</p>

<p>Bent grotesquely, its upper section smashed into the rooftop — it looked less like a steel tower and more like a broken toy.</p>

<p>Someone was standing on top of that folded radio tower.</p>

<p>Someone I should not be meeting alone.</p>
