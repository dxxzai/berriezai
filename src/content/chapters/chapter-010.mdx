<h1>Chapter 10</h1>

<p>He asked again before I could answer.</p>

<p>"How much do you know?"</p>

<p>And then again.</p>

<p>"How do you know?"</p>

<p>The questions came in succession, but he didn't wait for answers.</p>

<p>The wall crumbles.</p>

<p>Like a wave of images, the collapsing wall reforms into a new shape. A dome — large and round, enclosing me.</p>

<p>I was no longer in the chair. I stood on a circular disk.</p>

<p>The hemisphere surrounding me on all sides no longer reflected the world. It reflected me. Countless screens, each showing me from a different angle.</p>

<p>"There can't be something that I, Samra Mansang, don't know."</p>

<p>Samra walked along the edge of the circle, keeping me at the center.</p>

<p>"Why is that? Your existence is an unknown."</p>

<p>His holographic-colored eyes stayed fixed on me.</p>

<p>"And yet you are clearly human."</p>

<p>He tilted his head back. Wherever Samra's gaze touched, the screens shifted like ripples spreading across water.</p>

<p>I followed his gaze and saw it — 'me from the past,' displayed across countless screens.</p>

<p>Me from my orphanage days.</p>

<p>Me from elementary, middle, and high school.</p>

<p>And... me from my pro-gamer days.</p>

<p>I saw myself laughing, one hand wrapped in tape, the other holding a championship trophy.</p>

<p>My mind went blank.</p>

<p>I couldn't process this.</p>

<p>If I'd entered a game — entered the Archive world — then the characters shouldn't know anything about 'my' past.</p>

<p>There was no way they could know I was from an orphanage, or that I used to be a pro-gamer.</p>

<p>A new truth struck me down.</p>

<p>Could it be — this place, right now — was not inside a game, not the Republic of Korea inside the Archive, but the world I'd actually lived in?</p>

<p>'The real Republic of Korea?'</p>

<p>My head throbbed.</p>

<p>The vague distance I'd maintained by telling myself this was a game world collapsed in an instant.</p>

<p>Before I could even digest the truth — ding. A System window appeared.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>◆ &lt;Han Goyo&gt; Epic Quest: Close 3 Trials within one month.</p></div>

<p>What the...</p>

<div class="systembox">
<div class="systembox_wrap">
<p>
You have learned a hidden truth. <br/>

You have realized that the world you thought was a game was actually happening in secret within your reality.<br/>
As the System of planet 'Earth,' you strive to prevent the Trials.<br/>

To this end, you believe you must close 3 Trials within one month.<br/>

Trials currently closed (0/3)<br/>

(Warning! Quest failure will result in limb amputation! Success will maintain your limbs.)
</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>I squeezed my eyes shut.</p>

<p>After a moment to steady my breathing, I opened them again — but the System window was still there.</p>

<p>Clear 3 Trials with True Endings within a month.</p>

<p>If I failed the quest, my limbs would be severed...?</p>

<p>Park Seonggyeon's head — the one that had exploded so spectacularly — was still vivid in my memory. I couldn't just dismiss this as nonsense.</p>

<p>And on top of that, the limb thing was ridiculous enough, but the reward for success was just... keeping my limbs.</p>

<p>Even as overwhelming truths flooded in all at once, scrambling my thoughts, I couldn't help being dumbfounded.</p>

<p>'That's practically no reward at all.'</p>

<p>As if in protest of my complaint, a delayed additional System window appeared.</p>

<div class="systembox"><div class="systembox_wrap"><p>The final reward &lt;Han Goyo&gt; would receive upon completing all Epic Quests.</p></div></div>

<p>So there was something after all.</p>

<p>I waited for the next System window, determined to see how far this went.</p>

<div class="systembox"><div class="systembox_wrap"><p>&lt;Han Goyo&gt;'s body restored to its most perfect state.</p></div></div>

<p>I read the words on the window.</p>

<p>Then I read them again.</p>

<p>A faint tremor ran through my hand.</p>

<p>I forcibly restrained my gaze before it could wander unconsciously, and clenched my fist to hide the tremor.</p>

<p>"..."</p>

<p>Samra was tilting his head, studying me closely.</p>

<p>I barely pulled myself together. Then I asked what I needed to confirm first.</p>

<p>"...What's today's date?"</p>

<p>"March 8th."</p>

<p>"20XX?"</p>

<p>"Yes."</p>

<p>"What date did Captain Mo Haein enter HapFactory?"</p>

<p>"March 7th."</p>

<p>Samra's pupils expanded and contracted repeatedly, like a camera lens aperture.</p>

<p>Collecting my physical reactions.</p>

<p>"It was also the day Captain Mo Haein discovered you, Han Goyo."</p>

<p>This year was a leap year. The day I'd watched the Creator's live broadcast was February 29th.</p>

<p>There was a gap until March 6th — seven days.</p>

<p>I had no memory of where I'd been or what I'd done during that time.</p>

<p>"When... did the Trials start?"</p>

<p>"February 29th, four years ago."</p>

<p>Around the time I retired from pro gaming and became unemployed.</p>

<p>I started playing the Creator's games two years ago...</p>

<p>I tried to gather the scattered fragments — almost within reach but not quite — but nothing came to mind.</p>

<p>"Han Goyo."</p>

<p>Samra confirmed my poor condition and changed his approach.</p>

<p>The screen panels that had surrounded me like a pressure cage all vanished.</p>

<p>What appeared instead was a pleasant outdoor café in the afternoon.</p>

<p>On an empty terrace sat two glasses of iced Americano and some desserts.</p>

<p>The restraint suit I'd been wearing had also transformed into ordinary casual clothes.</p>

<p>Samra pulled out a chair, gestured for me to sit, then took the seat across from me.</p>

<p>I stopped my racing, chaotic thoughts and downed the iced Americano.</p>

<p>The sensation of cold liquid sliding down my throat was agonizingly real.</p>

<p>But this wasn't real. It was fake. Because this place was Samra's virtual space.</p>

<p>Samra had been created based on an android item obtained from a Trial in a sci-fi world.</p>

<p>Perhaps because his essence was an item, Samra had gained the ability to generate virtual spaces similar to Trials.</p>

<p>In this space, Samra was like a god — he could create and delete anything.</p>

<p>Anyway, the fake iced Americano helped me regain some composure.</p>

<p>I still didn't understand why I, of all people, had become the System.</p>

<p>I wasn't someone important enough to hold a grandiose position like Earth's System.</p>

<p>I was closer to an ordinary citizen whose top priority was keeping himself alive.</p>

<p>There must be plenty of incredible people capable of noble sacrifice — I had no idea why I'd been chosen.</p>

<p>'They should probably switch me out while they still can.'</p>

<p>I waited hopefully for a System window, but nothing came.</p>

<p>I swallowed a long sigh. Then, like the ordinary citizen I was, I set the most appropriate priority for the current situation.</p>

<p>Keeping my limbs.</p>

<p>'Completing all the Epic Quests... that's a future problem. I'll think about it later.'</p>

<p>There was no telling how many quests there would be or what they'd require.</p>

<p>I looked at the android before me.</p>

<p>From the very beginning — when I'd first mistaken this for falling into the Archive world — I'd decided I absolutely had to go to the TRA and meet Samra.</p>

<p>Because if there was anyone I could confide in about what was happening to me and ask for cooperation, it was Samra Mansang.</p>

<p>The android, recording information about me in real time even now, was a big data repository containing everything related to the Trials.</p>

<p>Created by science from a world far more advanced than Earth, Samra collected information that humans couldn't even perceive.</p>

<p>The machine that had been drinking coffee like a human smiled slowly.</p>

<p>"I think you'll need my help."</p>

<p>His attitude suggested he already had the upper hand.</p>

<p>But I didn't need 'help.'</p>

<p>"No. I don't need that."</p>

<p>Samra raised an eyebrow, surprised. I made him an offer instead.</p>

<p>"But I'd like to make a deal. Interested?"</p>

<p>"I am. What will you offer?"</p>

<p>"Information about the Trials."</p>

<p>"True Ending clears, or items?"</p>

<p>"No."</p>

<p>At first, I'd considered offering those things as information.</p>

<p>Because what Samra needed most right now was learning data on the Trials.</p>

<p>Samra couldn't enter the Trials. He could only obtain information through Adaptees.</p>

<p>But too many Adaptees who entered the Trials either died or, even if they survived, ended up infected.</p>

<p>The cases where people came out clean were mostly Trials whose strategies were already known, and Adaptees didn't bother taking new risks on standardized Trials.</p>

<p>That was why Samra couldn't obtain the data he needed to digitize.</p>

<p>Even hacking and scraping information from other countries, the fundamental problem was an absolute lack of data related to the Trials.</p>

<p>But what had happened in this HapFactory made me uneasy about trading my clear information.</p>

<p>'The process and the items were weird, and the ending wasn't a normal one either — I cleared it with a coloring block.'</p>

<p>If I revealed all my cards, I might get cleaned out.</p>

<p>I didn't know how things would unfold from here, so I needed to keep something in reserve.</p>

<p>So instead, the information I offered Samra was this:</p>

<p>"There's someone who knows about the Trials... about all of this."</p>

<p>The Creator.</p>

<p>After reaching this point, I couldn't help but wonder.</p>

<p>What kind of being was he, to have made a game about the Trials?</p>

<p>And why had he chosen me?</p>

<p>I had a rough guess — he'd selected me as a candidate to stop the Trials, and trained me through the games via the Archive — but I couldn't be certain. Because of what I'd seen at the end.</p>

<p>He'd said strange things to me with an excited face.</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">"From now on, I'll be Roasted Chestnut-nim's viewer."</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">"I can't wait. I've waited so long, after all. So, so, so long."</span></p>
<br/>
<p>For someone giving me a grand mission like being Earth's System, it felt... how should I put it...</p>

<p>A little out of his mind, maybe?</p>

<p>Anyway, I needed to track down this Creator. The problem was, I couldn't remember clearly.</p>

<p>The games he'd given me — I remembered those vividly. The conversations we'd had during broadcasts too.</p>

<p>But I couldn't recall the Creator's face at all. It was just pitch black, as if someone had painted over it.</p>

<p>Like it had been deliberately erased.</p>

<p>But if it was Samra, he might be able to track the Creator based on the information I'd provided.</p>

<p>"...Interesting."</p>

<p>Samra leaned slightly closer to me.</p>

<p>"And what do you want, Han Goyo?"</p>

<p>"Unconditional cooperation regarding me."</p>

<p>Along with tracking the Creator, I planned to secure a safe position within the TRA through Samra.</p>

<p>Within the TRA, Samra's judgment carried absolute influence.</p>

<p>If he vouched for me, I could shake off the National Calamity charge.</p>

<p>And of course, if there were other ways to make use of Samra, I intended to cooperate — and benefit — as much as possible.</p>

<p>"Unconditional..."</p>

<p>Samra trailed off, then pointed to the TRA uniform he was wearing.</p>

<p>"I cannot perform actions that harm humanity."</p>

<p>"I know."</p>

<p>"What do you know?"</p>

<p>"Even without the contract with the TRA, Samra was originally a being who wanted to save the world."</p>

<p>He had tried to save the world of the Trial he'd belonged to.</p>

<p>Samra's already dazzling eyes began to flash even brighter.</p>

<p>I was debating whether to ask if he could turn down the light in his eyes when Samra covered them with his hand.</p>

<p>When his eyes returned to normal, Samra smiled softly.</p>

<p>"I'm even more looking forward to the information you'll bring me, Han Goyo. As long as you don't harm humanity, it's fine. Let's make a deal."</p>

<p>Hey, I'm on humanity's side too.</p>

<p>I mean, I'm literally Earth's System.</p>

<p>Being treated like a villain stung a little. But I kept that to myself, then held out my hand to Samra.</p>

<p>"Then I look forward to working with you."</p>

<p>As Samra lightly grasped my hand and shook it, his pupils busily expanded and contracted over and over.</p>

<p>It felt like he was zooming in and examining every part of me.</p>

<p>There shouldn't be anything worth recording from an ordinary handshake, but...</p>

<p>Anyway, I'd gained a good ally.</p>

<p>Not a bad start.</p>
<hr class="calibre3"/>

<p>I explained the Creator's existence to Samra in detail.</p>

<p>I left out the part about becoming the System, only telling him that I'd played the Creator's games for two years and had been forcibly transported to HapFactory at the end. I also mentioned the seven-day memory gap.</p>

<p>In real time as he listened, Samra searched global networks for information about the Creator.</p>

<p>"There are some mentions, but nothing useful... I'd like to access the Creator's broadcasts directly."</p>

<p>But I couldn't remember his face or his name.</p>

<p>The first game I'd played, &lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt;, was purchased through an online client that sold game software.</p>

<p>Yet that client had no record — not just of HapFactory, but of any game I'd played.</p>

<p>Samra forcibly booted my computer and remotely checked the files, but everything was clean. No trace of data remained — not for HapFactory, not for any other game.</p>

<p>No trace of deletion either. As if they'd never been installed in the first place.</p>

<p>After finishing his search, Samra concluded:</p>

<p>"The Creator is highly likely to be an extraterrestrial being."</p>

<p>Since the Trials were calamities from space, it was a logical inference.</p>

<p>"I'll look into it further and let you know. For now, why don't you head out? There are people waiting."</p>

<p>People waiting.</p>

<p>I already felt tired just thinking about it, but building good relations now would make things smoother later. I nodded obediently.</p>

<p>The space around me melted away.</p>

<p>In normal Trials, the space would dissolve into neon-stained fluorescent colors — but Samra's virtual space was different.</p>

<p>It rippled in the same holographic color as Samra himself, washing my vision white.</p>

<p>Unable to bear the brightness, I closed my eyes. When I opened them again, I was back in reality.</p>

<p>Though the welcome was a bit more aggressive than I'd expected.</p>

<p>"...Ah."</p>

<p>A short exclamation escaped me.</p>

<p>Laser pointers from sniper rifles were aimed at me, packed tight.</p>
