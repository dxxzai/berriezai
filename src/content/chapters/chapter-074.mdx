<h1>Chapter 74</h1>


<p>Mo Haein and Kwak Hanmuk immediately sprang to their feet and drew their swords.</p>

<p>I rushed forward to open the door, but I was stopped.</p>

<p>A paladin’s massive white greatsword blocked my path.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk narrowed his eyes and spoke.</p>

<p>“Hold it.”</p>

<p>Mo Haein also shot a brief command at me.</p>

<p>“Trainee, wait.”</p>

<p>Leaving me behind, Kwak Hanmuk approached the door.</p>

<p>He had been about to kick it open but seemed to recall that business was set to begin the next day; instead, he opened it gently with his hand.</p>

<p>Creeak—the rusty hinges let out an unpleasant sound.</p>

<p>Outside the restaurant, the sun had fully set, leaving the world in darkness.</p>

<p>As the light spilled out from within, the figures lurking in the dark became visible.</p>

<p>Ella was clutching a blood-drenched Ellun, her face deathly pale.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk flinched and muttered.</p>

<p>“Shit... is that Lieutenant Colonel Je?”</p>

<p>Since Je Hyun-oh had headed to the dungeon, it was natural to suspect him first.</p>

<p>Mo Haein seemed to be thinking the same thing, but I knew better.</p>

<p>Je Hyun-oh wasn't the culprit.</p>

<p>Because this...</p>

<p>...was an event that occurred right before the ending.</p>

<p>It was the ‘Punishment of Infernum.’</p>

<p>It was an event that had a chance of triggering when the player was successfully managing both the restaurant and the dungeon raids.</p>

<p>It was an act of rage by Infernum, who foresaw that it would no longer be able to devour the player.</p>

<p>Unable to enjoy fine cuisine for so long, Infernum vented its fury by punishing its twin subordinates.</p>

<p>The image of the twins—each with a broken wing—standing before the dungeon and speaking to me flashed through my mind. </p>
<div class="bigbox2_pixel"><p>“Roasted Chestnut. The dishes you made were the best in the world. Keep honing your craft.”<br/>
“Roasted Chestnut is the greatest chef.”</p></div>

<p>No matter how many times I chose the dialogue option asking what was going on—sensing an atmosphere different from usual—the twins just repeated the same words.</p>

<p>And once I entered the dungeon and returned, the twins would be gone.</p>

<p>They couldn't be found anywhere in the village or the dungeon; traces of them would only appear after defeating the final boss, Infernum.</p>

<p>Inside Infernum’s ingredient storage.</p>

<div class="bigbox2_pixel"><p>Fire Dragon’s Red Horn (Small): A fresh horn from the Fire Dragon race. When ground into a powder and sprinkled on food, it adds amazing flavor. Its small size makes it less valuable than a standard horn.</p></div>

<p>Since the player faced no issues running the restaurant or clearing the dungeon, continuing the game would lead to the normal ending.</p>

<p>But for me, it wasn't the normal ending—it was the bad ending.</p>

<p>And now, seeing it in reality rather than as pixel art...</p>

<p>I gritted my teeth as I looked at the twisted right wing of Ellun.</p>

<p>At least Ella didn't seem to be injured.</p>

<p>‘It’s not time for that event to trigger yet.’</p>

<p>The conditions for Infernum’s "Punishment" event were clearing the uppermost level of the dungeon and having a restaurant reputation score of 800 or higher.</p>

<p>The event would trigger with a certain probability if the player paid the daily quota to Infernum but failed to send any food to the dungeon for ten consecutive days.</p>

<p>But right now, while I had cleared the dungeon quickly, that was all I’d done. I hadn't even opened the restaurant.</p>

<p>Infernum’s voice suddenly flashed through my mind.</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">“Yes... if I can eat you, it will be a rare treat...”</span> </p>
<br/>
<p>Perhaps the problem was that he viewed me as a special delicacy.</p>

<p>After all, in the game, there was never an instance where a character spoke such lines directly to the player.</p>

<p>Still, since only Ellun was injured, it didn't seem like a full-blown "punishment event" had been triggered yet.</p>

<p>There was still a chance.</p>

<p>I carefully called out to Ella.</p>

<p>“Ella-nim.”</p>

<p>Her trembling eyes darted past Kwak Hanmuk and fixed on me.</p>

<p>Ella called out to me, her voice sounding like a scream.</p>

<p>“Little Lamb!”</p>

<p>Holding the limp form of Ellun, Ella choked back sobs, unable to finish her sentence. Tears welled up in her large eyes.</p>

<p>I made a request to Kwak Hanmuk.</p>

<p>“Captain Kwak. Please let them inside.”</p>

<p>Before he could object, I quietly added:</p>

<p>“It is essential for clearing the True Ending.”</p>

<p>In truth, achieving the True Ending was impossible without the twins.</p>

<p>After all, the twins had to drive out Infernum and become the masters of the dungeon.</p>

<p>At the mention of necessity, Kwak Hanmuk glanced at Mo Haein.</p>

<p>She stared at the twins with an expressionless face before slowly turning to look at me.</p>

<p>“…I suppose we have no choice. If it’s for the True Ending.”</p>

<p>At Mo Haein’s words, Kwak Hanmuk sheathed his sword, looking slightly displeased.</p>

<p>I immediately ran over to Ella.</p>

<p>“Let me check on Ellun’s injuries.”</p>

<p>With a dazed look on her face, Ella handed Ellun over to me.</p>

<p>I laid him on the ground and examined his wings; the condition was poor.</p>

<p>The wing membranes were torn in several places, though fortunately, only one bone was broken.</p>

<p>As she gazed at his wretched state, Ella’s eyes darkened with despair. “What if Ellun never flies again…?”</p>

<p>“That won’t happen. We can definitely heal him, so don’t worry.”</p>

<p>As I reassured Ella, Dominic spoke from above me.</p>

<p>“Roasted Chestnut. Let me try using magic. I see I have a healing spell among my skills.”</p>

<p>It seemed he’d taken a liking to magic after defeating the boss in the dungeon, but that was absolutely out of the question.</p>

<p>Judging by the way he used magic, he might well end up turning Ellun into a metallic mechanical dragon in the name of healing him.</p>

<p>I also had to be careful, remembering how the system window had crashed twice because of Dominic since we entered Slutufle.</p>

<p>Fortunately, there was one viable treatment method available in the current situation.</p>

<p>‘I need to set the bone back in place first, though.’</p>

<p>I tried to ask the captains for help.</p>

<p>But Mo Haein had disappeared somewhere, and only Kwak Hanmuk was standing there, watching me.</p>

<p>“Where did Captain Mo go?”</p>

<p>“Said he was tired and went back up.”</p>

<p>I blinked in surprise, having never heard him say something like that before.</p>

<p>I found it hard to believe Mo Haein would do such a thing, but now wasn't the time to question it.</p>

<p>“Captain Kwak, please help me. I need to set this bone.”</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk ambled over and squatted down beside me.</p>

<p>He frowned as he looked at the broken wing.</p>

<p>“I’ve never set a wing bone before.”</p>

<p>“We just need to align that protruding part back into place.”</p>

<p>“Hmm… okay. I’ll set it on the count of three.”</p>

<p>“All right.”</p>

<p>With a tense expression, I held onto Ellun’s body. Ella held onto the other wing. Kwak Hanmuk carefully gauged the position of the bone before placing his hand upon it.</p>

<p>Then, without counting down, he snapped it right back into place.</p>

<p>“Aaaargh!”</p>

<p>Crack. As the bone reset, Ellun—who had previously been struggling to draw even a ragged breath—let out a sharp scream.</p>

<p>Ella and I hurriedly pinned down Ellun’s body as it thrashed reflexively.</p>

<p>“Y-y-you insolent…!”</p>

<p>Small sparks spat from Ella’s mouth.</p>

<p>Even as she glared furiously with pink eyes—her pupils narrowed into sharp slits—Kwak Hanmuk replied with utter nonchalance.</p>

<p>“It was going to hurt anyway.”</p>

<p>…He wasn’t wrong.</p>

<p>I asked Kwak Hanmuk to hold Ellun steady while I put on my raven-feather gloves.</p>

<p>Then, I ran to the brazier and retrieved a Flame Slime core.</p>

<p>I placed the core—seething with contained flames—onto Ellun’s damaged wing.</p>

<p>The moment it made contact, the slime core hissed, sending up a puff of smoke as it slowly melted down.</p>

<p>I spread the now-viscous, liquid slime evenly over the wing, much like an ointment.</p>

<p>I gathered the torn wing membrane with my hands; once the slime was applied, the edges bonded together.</p>

<p>As the liquid seeped in, Ellun’s breathing gradually began to steady.</p>

<p>After applying the entire Flame Slime core, the damaged wing had more or less regained its original shape.</p>

<p>Since I had merely reconnected the membrane, the line of the tear remained visible.</p>

<p>The broken bone needed stabilization, so with Kwak Hanmuk’s help, I folded the wing against the body.</p>

<p>Then, I wrapped it tightly with a makeshift bandage fashioned from a long strip torn from my apron.</p>

<p>Finally, I attached leather straps to the bandage to secure the wing firmly against the torso. It was a device designed to keep the wings from flapping.</p>

<p>Only after finishing that step could I finally catch my breath.</p>

<p>It was fortunate that I knew the treatment for bat wings.</p>

<p>I had simply looked it up after playing Sla2Plate, never imagining I’d actually use the information.</p>

<p>I offered some words of encouragement to Ella, who was weeping silently.</p>

<p>“Things will get better little by little.”</p>

<p>Probably.</p>

<p>The fact that Fire Dragons used Flame Slime cores as a sort of cure-all was just a snippet of information I’d picked up in the game; I had never actually tried it myself.</p>

<p>I didn't know what the outcome would be.</p>

<p>But for Ella’s sake, I spoke optimistically.</p>

<p>If all else fails, I could just buy a mechanical dragon wing to replace the right one.</p>

<p>That might actually look pretty cool. Just as I was entertaining this extreme idea, I heard a dejected voice.</p>

<p>“Little Lamb.”</p>

<p>Ella looked up at me, her wings drooping low.</p>

<p>“If we don’t have the Flame Slime core, we can’t cook, can we...?”</p>

<p>She knew, too, that the core was essential for roasting monster meat.</p>

<p>Of course, we could always go back to the dungeon to get another one, but the problem was the mountain of lizard tails that needed cooking right away.</p>

<p>If Flame Lizard tails weren't cooked on the day they were harvested, they would all spoil.</p>

<p>They were ingredients that required roasting over a fire to extend their shelf life.</p>

<p>“That... will be fine.”</p>

<p>“Why would it be fine? You’re lying to me!”</p>

<p>“Oh, no, that’s not it. You’re going to help me, aren't you, Ella-nim?”</p>

<p>“Me?”</p>

<p>I nodded in response to the bewildered Ella.</p>

<p>“Yes. Let’s roast some of those lizard tails together.”</p>

<p>“Haa... haa... Little lamb. Are you done yet?”</p>

<p>Ella asked me, panting heavily. Her face was flushed bright red.</p>

<p>Ella had been responsible for providing the heat to roast the 500 skewers.</p>

<p>Using the Fire Dragon’s breath to roast them made the process incredibly convenient and fast.</p>

<p>Since I hadn't been able to buy an extra hearth today, it would have taken ages using the kitchen's single existing hearth; I was lucky to have the Fire Dragon's help.</p>

<p>“Thank you for your hard work. They’re all done.”</p>

<p>I thought about offering her a skewer, but Ella ran off, saying she didn't even want to smell them anymore.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>11:00 PM</p></div>

<p>Checking the time, I picked up two skewers and left the kitchen.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk had an empty wooden skewer in his mouth.</p>

<p>Beside him sat a chest—separate from my inventory—used for storing items; it was packed tight with the twigs Je-Hyun-oh had trimmed off while shaping the landscaping trees.</p>

<p>While I grilled the skewers, Kwak Hanmuk fashioned wooden sticks for me out of those twigs.</p>

<p>Thanks to his nimble hands, I was able to grill the lizard tails without a hitch.</p>

<p>“Chestnut-ie, are they done?”</p>

<p>“Yes. Here, have some.”</p>

<p>I handed him the skewers I was holding in both hands and tentatively broached the subject.</p>

<p>“Um... I don’t think I should send the twins back to the dungeon. I was thinking of letting them sleep in my room and having them help out at the restaurant tomorrow...”</p>

<p>Perhaps the chopped steak had already built up some trust; Kwak Hanmuk accepted the lizard tail skewer without hesitation, took a bite, and answered while chewing.</p>

<p>“Suit yourself.”</p>

<p>I was actually surprised by how readily he gave his permission.</p>

<p>When I stared at him with wide eyes, he just shrugged.</p>

<p>“We went to the trouble of fixing those wings, so we might as well put them to use. The Lieutenant Colonel should be arriving soon; I’ll explain the situation, so you just go get some sleep, Al-bam.”</p>

<p>“...By the way, what about Captain Mo?”</p>

<p>“Captain Mo?”</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk paused mid-bite and let out a long sigh.</p>

<p>Gazing toward the second floor with a wistful look in his eyes, he spoke very slowly.</p>

<p>“He’s allergic to Fire Dragons.”</p>

<p>All the tension that had been coiled tight inside me instantly drained away.</p>

<p>Kwak Hanmuk chuckled and poked me with the empty wooden skewer.</p>

<p>“You only just got over your ‘butler allergy,’ so don’t go looking for Captain Mo—just get some sleep. Got it?”</p>

<p>“Yes...” Given how strongly she had spoken, it seemed best to just quietly go to sleep.</p>

<p>I needed to check on Ellun’s condition anyway.</p>

<p>I took Ella up to my room on the second floor. Ellun was fast asleep on the bed.</p>

<p>Ella and I carefully lay down at the edge of the bed, the pudding slime resting on my chest.</p>

<p>“Hey, Little Lamb.”</p>

<p>Ella, clutching her pillow, murmured while glancing at me.</p>

<p>“Thank you for your help.”</p>

<p>“You helped me too, Ella-nim. I survived thanks to that recovery potion.”</p>

<p>“For something so trivial...”</p>

<p>Ella chewed her lip before speaking again.</p>

<p>“The Lord broke Ellun’s wing. But since you healed it, the Lord might view you as an enemy from now on.”</p>

<p>“That goes for me, too.”</p>

<p>I couldn't exactly say that I wanted to roast Infernum until he was a well-done Fire Dragon steak, so I kept my answer simple.</p>

<p>Ella looked dazed at my reply.</p>

<p>It was quiet for a long while after that, and I was just mentally going over my plans for the next day...</p>

<p>When I heard a voice, barely more than a whisper.</p>

<p>“Why did you come to mind first?”</p>

<p>Enveloped in the darkness, Ella continued speaking, almost as if talking to herself.</p>

<p>“The moment Ellun’s wing was broken, you were the only one I could think of, Little Lamb. We’d only met two days ago, yet for some reason, I felt certain that you would help us...”</p>

<p>I turned over to face Ella.</p>

<p>She quickly covered her face with her pillow.</p>

<p>“Ella-nim.”</p>

<p>“...Why do you call me?”</p>

<p>I smiled faintly as I watched her peek out from behind the pillow. </p>

<p>“Just because.”</p>

<p>“…….”</p>

<p>“Thank you for coming to see me today.”</p>

<p>“…….”</p>

<p>“I used to imagine it sometimes. Wondering what it would have been like if, back then, you hadn’t just disappeared without a word but had asked me for help instead. I even looked up ways to treat it…”</p>

<p>“…….”</p>

<p>“Ah, it’s just… something like that happened to me in the past. Anyway, I was really happy that you came today, Ella.”</p>

<p>“…….”</p>

<p>“That’s all. Go to sleep now.”</p>

<p>I lay back down, looking straight up at the ceiling.</p>

<p>And I fell asleep, feeling the sensation of the pudding slime—which had slid down a bit—creeping back up onto my chest.</p>

<p>“You are a strange one, Little Lamb.”</p>

<p>I didn’t hear Ella’s quiet murmur.</p>
