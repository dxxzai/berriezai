<h1>Chapter 95</h1>


<p>I couldn't answer right away. After a moment, I slowly opened my mouth.</p>

<p>“I think it probably was me.”</p>

<p>As far as I remembered, I was the only viewer talking with the Creator back then.</p>

<p>Hearing my answer, CEO Cha muttered under his breath.</p>

<p>“Ha... fuck.”</p>

<p>Having suddenly become the person getting cursed at, I stared at him in confusion.</p>

<p>CEO Cha wasn't the type to swear often... was he...?</p>

<p>For a moment, I wondered if this was another case of me judging him solely based on my experiences in the game.</p>

<p>But putting that aside, what I was really wondering was what I'd done wrong in the first place.</p>

<p>Honestly, the only crime I'd committed was answering his question earnestly.</p>

<p>I cautiously watched his expression, and CEO Cha leaned back against the chair before speaking.</p>

<p>“Keep going.”</p>

<p>Can I really just keep talking? He's not going to suddenly pull out Daeil Yeorae, is he?</p>

<p>Still, judging by his personality, it didn't seem like he'd recklessly injure someone as valuable as me.</p>

<p>Gathering my courage, I continued.</p>

<p>“CEO Cha, were you... watching the final broadcast too?”</p>

<p>“It ended pretty lame. Suddenly he just said there weren't going to be any more games.”</p>

<p>“...!”</p>

<p>CEO Cha had been there for the Creator's final broadcast.</p>

<p>I already knew he'd played the Creator's games alongside me.</p>

<p>Samra had told me before we entered Clean3.</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">“The Creator's games were also played by Paseong's CEO.”</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">“The reason I prevented you from contacting Paseong's CEO is related to that as well. I am merely trying to choose the best option. I will not betray Han Goyo-ssi.”</span></p>
<br/>
<p>But that was all Samra had told me at the time.</p>

<p>Everything afterward had happened so quickly that there hadn't been time to talk in greater detail.</p>

<p>I'd expected that if I ever met CEO Cha, we'd end up talking about the Creator, but this still felt like I'd been caught off guard.</p>

<p>'I thought we'd talk about the Creator after he'd gotten me to sign the contract.'</p>

<p>I'd assumed whether I signed the contract or not would matter far more to CEO Cha.</p>

<p>“So you saw those weird bastards in the chat too.”</p>

<p>“The chat?”</p>

<p>When I asked in confusion, he answered as though it were obvious.</p>

<p>“They were always saying weird shit.”</p>

<p>In the chat?</p>

<p>I searched my memory.</p>

<p>Every time I watched the Creator's broadcasts, everyone in chat had always been having fun....</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">[Ifinishedtheendingnowstopthegamestopstopstop]</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">[ItHurtssomuch??Theblood?Wontstop???It?Hurts?Hurts???]</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">[Theyjustdiedrighttheyjustblewtheirheadopenrightinfrontofmediedright]</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">[ImsorryImsorryImsorryImsorryIwon'tdoitagainpleasespareme]</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">[Helpmehelpmehelpmehelpmehelpmehelpmehelpmehelpme]</span></p>
<br/>
<p>Waiting only for the Creator's games....</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">[Anyonepleasejustbringanyoneandletmeoutplease]</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">[Hihihikhehehehehehihihieveryonediednowitsmyturnhihihihihihihihi]</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">[IcantquittheresnoquitnoquitwhyisntthereIwanttostopplaying]</span></p>

<p><span class="flashback">[ThisisntagamethisismyrealbodyImreallygoingtodie]</span></p>
<br/>
<p>I thought they'd all been enjoying themselves...?</p>

<p>“......”</p>

<p>As I replayed those memories in my mind, it felt as though my blood had turned cold.</p>

<p>Why hadn't I realized the chat was abnormal?</p>

<p>There was no way anyone could look at those messages and think they were normal.</p>

<p>Yet until now, I'd never once questioned them. I'd simply accepted them as ordinary chat messages.</p>

<p>Even though I knew I needed to control my expression, I couldn't hide my agitation.</p>

<p>Watching my wavering eyes, CEO Cha spoke again.</p>

<p>“I heard you've been playing since two years ago and watched the final broadcast this year.”</p>

<p>Looking straight at me, he continued,</p>

<p>“I started four years ago.”</p>

<p>“...What?”</p>

<p>“And I only played for about six months.”</p>

<p>“Then... our timelines...”</p>

<p>“Don't match at all.”</p>

<p>For CEO Cha, the Creator's final broadcast had happened three and a half years ago.</p>

<p>My heart began pounding.</p>

<p>Once I accepted the premise that 'our timelines don't match' and reviewed the last four years of memories again, strange inconsistencies immediately started surfacing.</p>

<p>Looking back now, Seultuple was strange too.</p>

<p>Unlike the other games, Seultuple required five-player co-op, so I always matched with random players.</p>

<p>But the same fixed group of strangers somehow played with me every single time I logged in.</p>

<p>And this wasn't even a major online game—it was an indie game made by one person.</p>

<p>Unless they'd all been locked in front of computers waiting for me, that should've been impossible.</p>

<p>Yet back then, I'd never found it strange. I'd only focused on playing.</p>

<p>'What exactly is going on with the timeline...?'</p>

<p>As the question deepened, another chilling realization struck me.</p>

<p>For the past two years, I'd played every game the Creator gave me without stopping.</p>

<p>But if I added up the number of games and the time I'd spent playing them, there was no way all of it fit into just two years.</p>

<p>The game I'd played the most was the one where CEO Cha appeared as an NPC.</p>

<p>I'd played it one thousand times.</p>

<p>Even assuming each run took only four hours, that was four thousand hours.</p>

<p>Even if I played twenty-four hours a day without resting, it'd still take more than 166 days to finish it a thousand times.</p>

<p>And that wasn't even the only game I'd played.</p>

<p>The Archive games the Creator had given me numbered in the hundreds.</p>

<p>Two years wasn't nearly enough time to thoroughly play hundreds of games.</p>

<p>'No, more importantly, how could a single person even make hundreds of games in the first place? Even if they were pixel-art games.'</p>

<p>My heartbeat quickened.</p>

<p>Soon it was pounding so loudly it echoed in my ears.</p>

<p>What... did the Creator do to me?</p>

<p>What... am I?</p>

<p>My breathing became increasingly ragged until it was difficult just to breathe.</p>

<em><p>I felt like I couldn't think about it anymore.</p></em>

<p>“Roasted Chestnut.”</p>

<p>A gentle voice called to me.</p>

<p>Something soft patted my cheek, almost as though it were comforting me.</p>

<p>I gasped, drawing in a sharp breath as my mind cleared.</p>

<p>Then I saw CEO Cha with his hand hovering just before pressing the nurse call button.</p>

<p>I stared blankly at him.</p>

<p>“I'm okay....”</p>

<p>CEO Cha let out an incredulous scoff as he looked down at me.</p>

<p>Even I had to admit it wasn't a very convincing statement.</p>

<p>“......”</p>

<p>Slumping back into the chair, he lowered his gaze for a moment.</p>

<p>Then he let out a long breath.</p>

<p>It looked as though he was suppressing his irritation.</p>

<p>Seeing him stop himself from pressing the nurse call because of me and deliberately calm himself down felt strange.</p>

<p>This was the same CEO Cha who had torn open the side of the TRA building.</p>

<p>Realizing that someone like him had genuinely held back because I'd coughed up blood and collapsed left me feeling incredibly awkward.</p>

<p>Even throughout our conversation in the hospital room, he'd been remarkably gentle... for CEO Cha.</p>

<p>'Is this really CEO Cha? I mean, he was swinging Daeil Yeorae around, so it has to be.'</p>

<p>Just then, a chime rang out.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>You believe it is still too early for you to remember the past.<br/>
The Young God is still small and weak.</p></div>

<div class="systembox"><p>You wish to register new characters so that you may grow into a god as quickly as possible.</p></div>

<div class="systembox"><p>Would you like to register 'Seo Moonchan' and 'Mok Saehwa'?</p></div>

<p>The system window instantly calmed my chaotic mind.</p>

<p>Feeling a slight surge of irritation, I thought to myself,</p>

<p>'If you know something, then say it.'</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>Digest.</p></div>

<p>Seeing it send nothing but that single word annoyed me for a moment, until I remembered something it had said before.</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">[You believe that if you completely digest ■■■■, you may be able to register 'Cha■■'.]</span></p>
<br/>
<p>I still had no idea what it meant by <em>digest</em>.</p>

<p>But perhaps it meant it'd take time for my distorted memories to recover.</p>

<p>Pressuring the system window probably wouldn't accomplish anything.</p>

<p>I completely regained my composure.</p>

<p>“CEO Cha.”</p>

<p>Then, in a surprisingly calm voice, I said,</p>

<p>“As you said, our timelines are different, but it does seem that we played the same games. Would you mind telling me which games you played?”</p>

<p>CEO Cha listed the Creator's games he'd played.</p>

<p>None of them were the Trials I'd experienced.</p>

<p>'They probably haven't even hatched in reality yet.'</p>

<p>I'd already expected that he wouldn't know the games we'd shared.</p>

<p>It felt a little lonely that I alone remembered playing one of them a thousand times, but saying so would only make me seem even stranger, so I quietly kept it to myself.</p>

<p>He probably already thought I was weird enough as it was.</p>

<p>I continued talking with CEO Cha.</p>

<p>He also told me about how he'd played.</p>

<p>Unlike me, he hadn't bothered seeing every ending. He simply cleared the games as quickly as possible.</p>

<p>He didn't care whether it was the true ending or just a normal ending.</p>

<p>As soon as he cleared a game, he'd tell the Creator he was finished.</p>

<p>Apparently, the Creator never complained about that.</p>

<p>Also, far fewer real people had appeared as NPCs in CEO Cha's games than in mine.</p>

<p>Thanks to the games the Creator gave him, CEO Cha had been able to establish Paseong with relative ease.</p>

<p>He found Mok Saehwa and Seo Moonchan, who had both been NPCs in his games, and founded Paseong together with them, using his memories of the games to clear Trials.</p>

<p>That was why Paseong had grown so rapidly in its early days.</p>

<p>And every Trial he'd played in the games now belonged to Paseong.</p>

<p>“I tried tracking down the Creator myself, but every attempt failed.”</p>

<p>“And then Samra contacted you?”</p>

<p>CEO Cha gave a small nod.</p>

<p>“Samra said the Creator had been confirmed to be an Outer God beyond Earth.”</p>

<p>Outer Gods....</p>

<p>It was a concept connected to CallOVo, where Dominic had once stayed.</p>

<p>Thinking about it, the DeZonDeal zombie virus had felt similar too—there were many ways the Trials resembled reality.</p>

<p>Deciding I'd ask Samra more about the Outer Gods later, I asked the question I'd been most curious about.</p>

<p>“Then how did you end up playing the Creator's games in the first place, CEO Cha?”</p>

<p>The CEO Cha I knew wasn't the type to play indie pixel-art games or seek out a game developer's livestreams.</p>

<p>You could tell just from the way he'd played.</p>

<p>He'd never been particularly interested in the Creator's games.</p>

<p>He'd simply played each one like homework whenever it was handed to him.</p>

<p>I couldn't understand why someone like him had ended up playing them at all.</p>

<p>But CEO Cha had no intention of answering.</p>

<p>“Is this an interrogation?”</p>

<p>“No....”</p>

<p>I immediately shrank back.</p>

<p>I'd thought he was being surprisingly cooperative for CEO Cha, but it seemed I shouldn't push any further.</p>

<p>Still, even if I stopped asking about the Creator, there was one thing I absolutely wanted to confirm.</p>

<p>I figured he'd answer at least this much.</p>

<p>“CEO Cha. Um... sorry, but may I ask just one last thing?”</p>

<p>I spoke carefully.</p>

<p>“Was the reason you were looking for me because of the Creator?”</p>

<p>The man who'd been answering me without hesitation until now simply stared at me in silence.</p>

<p>The silence stretched on.</p>

<p>Without looking away, I waited for his answer.</p>

<p>Finally, he spoke.</p>

<p>“You think that's why?”</p>

<p>But it wasn't an answer.</p>

<p>'How would I know?'</p>

<p>I grumbled inwardly since there was no way I could tell CEO Cha to answer properly.</p>

<p>Then, without warning, he pulled Daeil Yeorae out of his inventory.</p>

<p>“So, Han Goyo.”</p>

<p>Stretching out one arm, CEO Cha drove the white sword into the floor.</p>

<p>“Are you going to sign with TRA?”</p>
