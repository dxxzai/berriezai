<h1>Chapter 2</h1>
<p>I was just as stunned as the other person. For two reasons.</p>

<p>First — Mo Haein.</p>

<p>Mo Haein was one of the main characters in the Archive, the connecting world that linked the creator's games together.</p>

<p>The Archive was a story that began when a calamity suddenly struck Earth one day.</p>

<p>At midnight on February 29th, KST, meteor showers poured down across the entire world.</p>

<p>Meteor showers that had never been predicted by astronomical observation.</p>

<p>The sudden meteor showers were like a festival of light. Humans marveled at the beautiful spectacle — but...</p>

<p>This was a calamity from outer space.</p>

<p>The calamity that crashed to Earth hatched in random order and form, creating spaces from another dimension.</p>

<p>Those otherworldly spaces had rules set up that were nearly identical to games.</p>

<p>The structure was just like a game: collect items, complete quests, clear the stage.</p>

<p>If you didn't clear it within a set time, the calamity would explode and mutate the surrounding area into the same form as that calamity.</p>

<p>Spreading an infectious virus that mutated both living and non-living things.</p>

<p>It was a calamity in the truest sense — inexplicable by modern science.</p>

<p>Humans considered the calamity from space a test from God and named it the 'Trial.'</p>

<p>With the hatching of the Trials, humans also began to bloom with special talents.</p>

<p>Beings who could see the System window appeared.</p>

<p>They were called 'Adaptees,' and they could clear Trials through the System window.</p>

<p>In the Archive world, the Player bloomed as an Adaptee and entered various Trials.</p>

<p>The setup was that each time you entered a different Trial, you played a different genre of game.</p>

<p>Mo Haein was a soldier in the Trial Response Agency, an external agency under the Republic of Korea's Agency of National Defense.</p>

<p>Captain Mo Haein, an elite of the TRA, would either appear to help the Player during a Trial, or show up at the ending to talk with the Player.</p>

<p>A character I'd only ever seen in pixels was now alive and moving right before my eyes — of course I was shocked.</p>

<p>And second — the System window.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>'Mo Haein' is a registrable character. Would you like to register?</p></div>

<p>I stared at the System window glowing with a hazy light, and recalled what I'd seen right before losing consciousness.</p>
<br/>
<p><span class="flashback">[You are the System of planet 'Earth']</span></p>
<br/>
<p>A message only visible to the Archive's Adaptees.</p>

<p>The thing that guided quests in Trials and sent out various notifications — what they called the System.</p>

<p>I was that System...?</p>

<p>This was something I'd never experienced even after playing countless Archive games.</p>

<p>Nothing made sense, but I had one strong guess.</p>

<p>Maybe I'd fallen into the game itself...</p>

<p>Like that game-possession thing that shows up all the time in subculture media.</p>

<p>The headache that had barely subsided started throbbing again at the sheer absurdity of the situation.</p>

<p>For now, I ignored the suspiciously glowing registration window. There was no way I was registering something I didn't even understand.</p>

<p>When I kept ignoring it, the registration window slid away on its own.</p>

<p>I thought it was finally gone — but then a new window popped up.</p>

<div class="systembox"><p>◆ Han Goyo (Roasted Chestnut) has entered &lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt;!</p></div>

<div class="systembox">
    <div class="systembox_wrap">
        <p>
        ◆ &lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt; Main Quest: Clear the murderous toy factory with a True Ending.<br/>
        You have entered your first Trial as the System.<br/>
        You must prove the System's abilities by achieving a True Ending clear.<br/>
        Survival upon quest success!<br/>
        Consequences of failure are unknown.<br/>
        However, you have a feeling that something unpleasant will happen to a loser.
        </p>
    </div>
</div>

<p>...What the hell is this.</p>

<p>I did my best to keep my expression under control after reading the System window.</p>

<p>While I was trying not to show any weirdness, I heard voices talking.</p>

<p>"Lieutenant Park, there were no entry application for HapFactory, right?"</p>

<p>"Absolutely none. I checked multiple times."</p>

<p>"Then it must be a civilian."</p>

<p>Click-clack. Mo Haein moved her claw hand and looked at me.</p>

<p>Attached to her left arm was a plastic toy arm.</p>

<p>The toy arm with a claw like a crane game — it was an item you could get in &lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt;, and it was also the thing that had just choked me.</p>

<p>Between the System window that had appeared before my eyes and the toy arm that was way too high-quality to be special effects makeup...</p>

<p>While I was dizzy from the realization that I was really inside a game, Mo Haein looked me up and down.</p>

<p>"You're kind of strange?"</p>

<p>She tilted her head, and her bobbed hair swayed.</p>

<p>I knew why she was eyeing me suspiciously.</p>

<p>It was extremely rare for a civilian to get caught up in a regularly managed Trial.</p>

<p>There were mercenaries who took commissions to clear Trials, and smugglers who snuck in coveting the items you could get from them — but almost nobody entered alone like this.</p>

<p>A Trial was a trial in every sense.</p>

<p>You had to risk your life, so entering with at least two people for backup was standard.</p>

<p>Smugglers had worse equipment than the government or private companies, so they usually came in groups of a dozen or more to make up for it with numbers.</p>

<p>Of course, for me, who'd played it as a game, solo entry was the default.</p>

<p>Either way, I got the feeling I wouldn't be treated well if I just stood there. I put on a scared face.</p>

<p>"Um... where is this...?"</p>

<p>I was genuinely confused about the situation, so the expression came naturally without much acting.</p>

<p>Mo Haein, who'd been weighing me on some mental scale, raised an eyebrow.</p>

<p>Then she gave an answer that would've made me faint if I were a civilian who really didn't know anything.</p>

<p>"A toy factory full of crazy murderous toys."</p>

<p>I gasped at the blunt answer, and the man next to me snickered.</p>

<p>"It's true there are killers, but don't worry. We'll get you out safe."</p>

<p>He said 'we're soldiers, after all,' and flashed his military ID.</p>

<p>"Trial Response Agency...?"</p>

<p>"Under the Agency of National Defense. It's new, so you probably haven't heard of it."</p>

<p>He was saying whatever because apparently civilians' memories got wiped once they left the Trial anyway.</p>

<p>I nodded at the man's words while checking their work uniforms.</p>

<div class="fieldset"><p>Employee: Sand</p></div>
<div class="fieldset"><p>Employee: Sample</p></div>

<p>Mo Haein wore an orange work uniform with 'Sand' on her name tag. The man wore a yellow one with 'Sample' on his.</p>

<p>But Mo Haein's name tag was a little different.</p>

<p>The smiley face on her tag wasn't smiling.</p>

<p>Its mouth was a straight line instead of a curve — a shape that didn't suit &lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt; at all.</p>

<p>The smiley on the name tag was a kind of life counter.</p>

<p>In &lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt;, or HapFactory for short, you got three chances. Every time you made a fatal mistake, the smiley's expression changed.</p>

<p>From happy smile to blank face, and then to sad smile with the corners of the mouth turned down.</p>

<p>The sad smile state was the last chance.</p>

<p>If you messed up there, it was game over immediately.</p>

<p>I couldn't believe a character like Mo Haein had already lost a life this early.</p>

<p>"What was that loud noise just now?"</p>

<p>I asked in a timid voice, as if testing the waters.</p>

<p>Honestly, what I really wanted to ask was what the hell she'd done — but unfortunately, I didn't get an answer.</p>

<p>"None of a civilian's business."</p>

<p>Mo Haein cut me off coldly, then pulled something from her inventory and tossed it at me.</p>

<p>I caught it on reflex — it was the same type of toy arm Mo Haein was wearing.</p>

<div class="systembox"><div class="systembox_wrap"><p>Cherry Picker: You can unleash the power of Happy Smile! Let's have a happy time together!</p></div></div>

<p>"Put it on."</p>

<p>At Mo Haein's words, the man's face twisted with irritation. He clearly didn't like the idea of giving a valuable item to some clueless civilian.</p>

<p>Didn't matter to me.</p>

<p>"I'm okay."</p>

<p>I was going to refuse the item anyway.</p>

<p>"I don't really know what's going on, so shouldn't you experts use it? It's kind of scary too."</p>

<p>I said the flashlight at my waist would probably be enough, and clenched my fist to show I'd follow along from behind.</p>

<p>The man looked satisfied with my answer. Mo Haein narrowed her eyes like she was looking at some weirdo.</p>

<p>I refused the item because of the True Ending.</p>

<p>HapFactory had multiple endings, and the conditions for getting the True Ending were especially tricky.</p>

<p>First, you weren't supposed to wear any of the toy limbs you get in the game.</p>

<p>As you progressed through the game, NPCs would give you toy arm and leg items one by one.</p>

<p>Players would naturally equip them because it 'seemed' essential to the game.</p>

<p>They were incredibly convenient items — but if you got greedy and replaced all your limbs with toys, you'd immediately get the Bad Ending.</p>

<p>The ending where you became a toy worker at HapFactory and worked in the factory forever.</p>

<p>Mo Haein should've known this much.</p>

<p>But seeing her wearing the toy arm, she probably didn't know about the True Ending.</p>

<p>For now, I planned to follow Mo Haein and the man while meeting the True Ending conditions.</p>

<p>It wasn't just because of the System window's weird threat.</p>

<p>If I can only see one ending, it has to be the True Ending.</p>

<p>I was a hardcore happy ending fan.</p>

<p>In games, the True Ending was usually the happy ending.</p>

<p>Even when I played Archive games, after seeing all the endings, I always finished with the True Ending.</p>

<p>I couldn't rest easy unless I ended the game seeing the characters happy.</p>

<p>I'm curious about that man too.</p>

<p>I had a pretty good memory.</p>

<p>As much as I loved the Archive, I remembered even the small details — but the man with the nickname 'Sample' was new to me.</p>

<p>I'd never seen him in any Trial or in the cookie cutscenes after the endings.</p>

<p>It wasn't that strange.</p>

<p>Died in a Trial, trapped forever, or survived but got infected by the virus and quarantined. Either way, it was common.</p>

<p>The man named Sample was probably a character who'd disappeared from the Archive world before I even registered him.</p>

<p>Mo Haein stared at me and spoke.</p>

<p>"Lieutenant Park, you wear it."</p>

<p>"Yes, ma'am!"</p>

<p>The man quickly took the toy arm and equipped it on his right arm.</p>

<p>As soon as he brought the item over his arm, his human arm vanished and the toy arm took its place.</p>

<p>He clicked the claw proudly.</p>

<p>"If you want to live, just follow me."</p>

<p>Mo Haein clicked her tongue without a word and walked ahead.</p>

<p>I followed behind her alongside the man. Quietly, I moved only my eyes to the side.</p>

<p>A Smiley was embedded in the wall.</p>

<p>The Smiley with a smiling face was the main toy of &lt;Happy Smile Factory&gt;.</p>

<p>The colorful plastic toy doll had the same claw hands as the items provided to players.</p>

<p>When you first entered the factory, you'd encounter an NPC Smiley — but unlike the mass-produced 15cm toys, this NPC Smiley was 1 meter tall.</p>

<p>Like the size of a small child.</p>

<p>You'd fight the Smiley later, but for now it was just exchanging a few lines and getting the toy arm item.</p>

<p>I had no idea why they'd attacked and even lost a life.</p>

<p>Given that Mo Haein didn't look too happy, I guessed the man had messed up somehow.</p>

<p>The Smiley, its head embedded in the wall, had its toy arms and legs dangling limply on the floor.</p>

<p>A crackling mechanical sound came from the Smiley.</p>

<em><p>"S-Smiley — welcomes-you — to-the-ha-happy-smile-factory — let's — be-happy-happy-together—..."</p></em>

<p>It wasn't exactly a pretty sight.</p>

<p>I'd gotten kind of attached to that NPC while playing — seeing it half-broken in person bothered me.</p>

<p>Like I felt the Smiley would visit my dreams tonight.</p>

<p>Unlike me, the man and Mo Haein didn't seem to care. The man spoke to me with a friendly smile.</p>

<p>"Should we introduce ourselves! Park Seonggyeon. Call me Lieutenant Park."</p>

<p>"Han Goyo."</p>

<p>"That person is Captain Mo Haein..."</p>

<p>"Lieutenant Park. You on a field trip?"</p>

<p>"Sorry."</p>

<p>Park Seonggyeon apologized quickly and shut his mouth, but rolled his eyes in annoyance.</p>

<p>Seeing that, I had a pretty good idea why I didn't know his character.</p>

<p>He must've caused an accident in a Trial and died.</p>

<p>If he was unlucky, maybe the day of that accident was today.</p>

<p>While Park Seonggyeon walked with a stiff face, I bent down like I was tying my shoelaces.</p>

<p>I scooped up a handful of the candies piled up here and there.</p>

<p>Candies with a hexagonal nut shape drawn on the wrapper.</p>

<div class="systembox"><div class="systembox_wrap"><p>Nut Candy: Clickety-clack, repairing toys...</p></div></div>

<p>Nut Candies, easily found all over the map, were HapFactory's recovery items.</p>

<p>They supposedly tasted like nuts when eaten — but unfortunately, I couldn't try them myself.</p>

<p>Because they could only be used while wearing a toy arm or leg.</p>

<p>As I passed by the Smiley, I casually dropped a candy onto its claw-shaped hand.</p>
